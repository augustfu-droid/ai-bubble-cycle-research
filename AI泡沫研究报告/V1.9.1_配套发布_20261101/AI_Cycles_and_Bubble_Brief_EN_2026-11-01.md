Daduizhang | V1.9.1 | 2026-11-01

<!-- BODY_START -->

Research cutoff: October 31, 2026 (Beijing time) | Edited: November 1, 2026

**I see the first half of 2027 through the first half of 2028 as the main risk window for the current AI capital bubble to burst on a broader scale.** Here, a "burst" means sustained valuation repricing across multiple entities, corroborated by pressures on investment, financing and recovery of capital from assets. A single stock-price decline cannot be counted as a successful forecast, nor would a bubble burst mean AI technology has lost its value.

The following is the author's subjective forecast, registered afresh on November 1 using evidence available through October 31, 2026. It is not a date precisely calculated by a model, and does not reinstate the probabilities or forecast declines in earlier editions:

| Scenario and possible timing | Triggers and transmission | Conditions that would change my judgment |
|---|---|---|
| **Baseline: H1 2027 to H1 2028** | Collections lag investment, depreciation on assets entering service and firm payment commitments increase, and downward revisions to returns affect valuations and investment | Cash catches up with investment, payback from older assets holds up and pressure stops accumulating |
| Earlier: November 2026 to Q1 2027 | A major customer's payments or refinancing run into trouble first, with procurement, collateral and guarantees spreading the shock | Financing and payments recover, without risk spreading across entities |
| Delayed amplification: H2 2028 to H1 2029 | Financing extends expansion, but new firm obligations still outpace collections, leaving more capital unrecovered | Construction slows and shortfalls narrow, so the extended cycle begins to help absorb investment |
| Absorption, avoiding a full-scale bubble burst: observe 2027-2029 | Continued payments and efficiency gains absorb earlier investment, while operating cash improves | Renewals, cash or financing weaken again, while investment once more runs out of control |

**If the baseline's repricing across multiple entities and transmission through funding and operations have not appeared by June 30, 2028, that window must be recorded as unfulfilled. It cannot automatically be rolled into the next row and counted as a correct forecast.** The four paths are not mutually exclusive and cannot be added into probabilities; the absorption scenario can mean that a full-scale bubble burst is avoided.

**Since April, I have consistently argued that "delay amplifies risk." My central judgment remains this: if cash receipts from AI commercialization fail to keep pace with continuing investment, while companies keep locking in the next round of equipment, lease and financing obligations, a prolonged cycle can accumulate greater pressure to recover capital. As GPUs enter service, depreciation also begins to affect profit. Growth in application revenue does not automatically cover both sets of costs.**

I also acknowledge that AI has real uses and that commercialization is expanding. Current evidence already shows construction cash shortfalls at some groups, but it does not justify concluding that all AI companies are loss-making or that every project will fail to pay back. My assessment of the current state is divergence among companies, with funding and earnings pressure accumulating at some; an industry-wide simultaneous collapse of demand and financing has not yet occurred.

My investment stance is consequently selective participation: first establish who pays and who retains the profit, then ask whether the purchase price leaves room for error. Application progress deserves more research attention, but it does not automatically justify higher valuations for all models, compute providers and related stocks. This brief uses the detailed report's evidence and cutoff. It adds no new facts from November and assigns no uncalibrated probability to a crash.

## 1. Why I Still Emphasize "Delay Amplifies Risk"

Three cloud providers offer the clearest comparison. In Microsoft's FY27Q1 ended September 30, operating cash was USD 58 billion. Deducting USD 39.367 billion in cash asset purchases/construction left USD 18.633 billion; after a further USD 1 billion in finance-lease principal, the balance was still USD 17.633 billion. [Microsoft results](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/Investor/earnings/FY-2027-Q1/press-release-webcast)

Alphabet's Cloud business generated revenue of USD 30.709 billion and operating profit of USD 11.121 billion in 2026Q3. Group operating cash for the quarter was USD 42.290 billion, against USD 51.355 billion in cash asset purchases/construction, a difference of negative USD 9.065 billion. Over the trailing twelve months, however, the same measure remained positive at USD 19.747 billion. [Alphabet results](https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_financials/2026/q3/2026q3-alphabet-earnings-release.pdf)

Amazon's AWS business generated revenue of USD 45.984 billion and operating profit of USD 17.773 billion in 2026Q3. For the trailing twelve months ended September 30, group operating cash of USD 168.3 billion less net cash asset purchases/construction of USD 210.657 billion produced free cash flow of negative USD 42.357 billion. [Amazon results](https://ir.aboutamazon.com/news-release/news-release-details/2026/Amazon-com-Announces-Third-Quarter-Results/default.aspx)

These periods and definitions cannot be mixed and added together, and cloud segment profit is not the same as returns on the group's AI projects. Together, they show that demand growth is real and that construction absorbs real cash. Evidence of supply-chain shipments, profits and some positive cash generation must also be acknowledged; upstream profits do not establish payback for every downstream project.

My judgment is that capital investment increasingly requires a concrete explanation of how it will be recovered. Construction requires upfront payment for assets used over many years, so a single quarter of negative cash flow does not establish investment failure. But contractual, lease and debt payments do not disappear because the potential future market is large. If investment persistently outpaces sustainable operating cash, new financing will affect interest expense, dilution and returns per share. Continued access to financing shows only that funding has been extended; it cannot substitute for a project's operating payback.

"Current AI revenue cannot cover cash flow" is my shorthand for this recovery pressure. The stricter test begins with customer collections covering necessary operations to generate operating cash, then asks whether that cash can cover construction and payments falling due that are not already included in operating cash. Revenue is not cash, and group cash is not AI-project cash. Existing financial reports support the presence of shortfalls at some groups, but do not support an aggregate industry-wide shortfall attributable specifically to AI. If revenue is delayed while payments are not, or companies commit to new projects before recovering investment in existing ones, waiting will tie up more capital. That is the mechanism behind my judgment; a further period of rising markets alone cannot establish that risk must have increased.

GPU depreciation tests whether earnings can bear the cost of the assets. Costs begin to be allocated once equipment is ready for use. More assets entering service, falling rental prices or insufficient utilization can all make depreciation pressure more pronounced. Older equipment still operating does not mean it can recover its cost at the original price; a new chip generation does not mean older equipment immediately becomes worthless. Rental income, effective utilization, maintenance investment, economic life and residual value should be checked for each equipment batch.

Depreciation is an accounting expense, not another current-period payment for the equipment. Free cash flow already deducts cash asset purchases/construction, so full depreciation cannot be deducted again. A change in accounting useful life changes the allocation of future expenses; impairment requires separate evidence of a trigger. We cannot begin by assuming all GPUs will be scrapped after three years. Longer depreciation periods can temporarily alter the timing of profit, but cannot recover capital already spent. Adding depreciation back in operating cash flow also does not eliminate future equipment replacement spending.

## 2. Microdramas Bring Video Models Closer to Production Budgets; Competition Will Still Determine Who Captures Profit

I give microdramas greater weight because there is already payment and procurement, not just an audience willing to watch novel videos. Duiba separately reported RMB 222.629 million in AI-microdrama revenue in the first half of 2026, while the group still recorded a loss of RMB 25.326 million over the same period. The former establishes that revenue has occurred; the latter cannot establish a standalone loss or net margin for the microdrama segment. [Duiba interim report](https://yun.duiba.com.cn/corp/report/content/1/2026%E4%B8%AD%E6%9C%9F%E5%A0%B1%E5%91%8A.3f87c290d81d43e1b87da58f7eaf9818.pdf)

Both China and the United States have models combining audience payments and advertising. Free distribution and content procurement in China, and paid apps and cross-border customer acquisition in the United States, operate under different payment and competitive conditions. The overall live-action microdrama market, AI-assisted production, primarily AI-generated works and model-tool revenue must be kept separate. The same finished production can be watched repeatedly, so viewing minutes or audience counts cannot simply be multiplied into a video-generation bill. Time spent in mobile apps also cannot directly establish that films are being displaced across all viewing platforms.

The more meaningful upstream demand from microdramas is studios' continuing production of new dramas, advertising assets, subsequent seasons and localized versions. Customers are not buying unlimited video; they are buying shots that can meet acceptance deadlines and be distributed. Higher acceptance rates and less rework benefit producers, but may reduce generation purchases for the same amount of finished footage. If new genres and new customers expand fast enough, total procurement may increase instead. Which force dominates depends on net paid output, realized prices and full costs.

The three types of vendors also have different paths to profit:

- **Seedance:** External model services may generate direct fees, while ByteDance's internal content and advertising businesses may gain efficiency. But internal usage is not new external revenue for the group, and Hongguo's growth does not mean Seedance captures all its procurement. This review did not obtain segment disclosures sufficient to independently recalculate Seedance's revenue, gross profit and training investment.
- **MiniMax:** Hailuo, enterprise APIs and open-weight licensing can generate different forms of revenue from professional production. In the first half of 2026, group revenue was USD 116.573 million and gross profit USD 20.813 million, against R&D expense of USD 296.870 million. Existing gross profit was far from covering R&D for the period. The open platform and enterprise services accounted for 63.4% of revenue and included multiple modalities; this cannot all be counted as video or microdrama revenue. Open weights may also broaden adoption while shifting some purchases of hosted services to customers' own compute. [MiniMax interim results](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0826/2026082600680.pdf)
- **Kling:** Revenue exceeded RMB 850 million in 2026Q2, showing that video tools already generate fees at scale. But the company did not separately disclose complete video profit or microdrama revenue in that release. Kling's external subscriptions and API fees should be accounted for separately from internal advertising and content efficiencies at Kuaishou. [Kuaishou Q2 results](https://ir.kuaishou.com/zh-hans/news-releases/news-release-details/kuaishoukejifabu2026niandierjidujizhongqiweijingshenhecaiwuyeji)

I place more weight on the operating ability to deliver repeatedly, control licensed IP and collect payments from users than on "knowing how to use a particular model." Once generation becomes cheaper per second, savings may be consumed by bidding for advertising traffic, unsuccessful productions, rework and platform revenue shares. Once procurement interfaces offer multiple models, underlying suppliers may also lose pricing power. Microdramas expand the potential to charge, but have not resolved how profit will be distributed across the industry.

## 3. Coding Requires a New Market Model and a New Way to Calculate Savings

The early research multiplied 36.5 million professional developers by a monthly fee of USD 30 and twelve months, producing USD 13.14 billion. This arithmetic is valid under its assumptions, but **presenting it as a hard ceiling for the entire market was wrong. V1.5 already withdrew that ceiling; this edition does not present the correction as a new discovery.** The original draft also contradicted its "absolute ceiling" with higher-price scenarios and made a unit error by using "USD 13.1 billion" and "$131B" interchangeably.

Usage-based billing, long tasks, code review and nonprofessional uses mean one person's budget is no longer limited to a single low-price seat. GitHub Copilot's move to usage-based billing in June 2026 is concrete product evidence of this change. But annualized revenue, active-user figures and group revenue from different tools cannot simply be combined into a market total free of double counting. This report still has not established that actual revenue from coding alone exceeds the old calculation. [GitHub billing explanation](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-06-01-updates-to-github-copilot-billing-and-plans/)

I expect the labor impact to appear first in hiring, departures that are not replaced and standardized outsourcing. A company avoiding layoffs does not mean its planned hiring has not declined; code growth does not mean payroll can fall in the same proportion. The revised Chen-Stratton study of 655 firms found that growth at the generation stage weakened as it passed through to delivery, and detected no significant change in engineer employment at the sampled firms. This is an observational study, and "not detected" cannot be interpreted as ruling out workforce reductions. [Study of enterprise organization](https://fion.ac/jellyfish.pdf)

Stanford's study using ADP data found that employment among workers aged 22-25 in occupations with high AI exposure lagged the trajectory of peers in low-exposure occupations by 19%, with reduced hiring accounting for much of the divergence. This is not "19% of programmers were laid off," is not an identified net causal effect of AI, and cannot be applied as a labor displacement rate for China. [Stanford labor study](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf)

The detailed report uses two sets of author assumptions to illustrate budget differences. Neither is a company measurement or an industry average, and both require delivery of equal quality. A ten-person US team releases a net 2,400 hours in steady state, valued at USD 240,000 using internal costs. Deducting USD 40,000 for tools, services and a risk budget leaves net capacity value of USD 200,000. If everyone remains employed, there are no new cash receipts and no purchases are canceled, cash spending and the risk budget instead increase by USD 40,000. A net benefit of USD 140,000 arises only if the company actually avoids a full-year hire costing USD 180,000 and the released capacity covers that person's work.

In the eight-person China example, a net 900 hours are released in steady state, with an internal cost value of RMB 120,000. Deducting RMB 90,000 leaves net capacity value of RMB 30,000. If there are genuinely 900 hours of cancelable outsourcing at RMB 220 per hour, and the internal team can complete equivalent work, the avoided payment is RMB 198,000, leaving a net benefit of RMB 108,000 after deducting RMB 90,000. Net capacity value cannot be added to the benefit from this alternative use of the same capacity.

The USD 40,000 and RMB 90,000 both include risk budgets, so they do not consist entirely of cash already paid. Training, integration and parallel validation also occur in the first year, so steady-state benefits cannot be applied directly. Lower-wage teams can benefit, but they have a greater need to control review and tool costs or find spending that can actually be canceled. Of every RMB 100 a customer saves, only a portion will eventually become supplier revenue, and competition will determine its allocation.

## 4. Other Applications Increase the Importance I Place on Existing Payment Relationships

AI commercialization extends beyond coding and video. AlphaSense disclosed company ARR above USD 700 million in 2026Q2, supporting the presence of paid scale in knowledge workflows. This is a company-reported annualized recurring metric that includes the value of content, search and the platform; it is not quarterly revenue or incremental generative-AI revenue. [AlphaSense original disclosure](https://www.alpha-sense.com/press/alphasense-named-the-only-leader-in-market-and-competitive-intelligence-platforms/)

Duolingo generated USD 78.63 million in free cash flow under its own definition in 2026Q2. This establishes that an education product containing AI features can generate positive cash, but does not allow all course revenue or profit to be attributed to AI. [Duolingo Q2 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026053603/duol-20260630.htm)

In contrast, Character.ai entered a court-supervised sale process in October despite already having subscription and advertising revenue. The service was still operating at the time; it must not be described as already shut down or sold. This shows that user value, monetization and shareholder returns can diverge, but does not invalidate all companion products. [Character.ai official announcement](https://blog.character.ai/an-update-on-character-ai/)

My research priorities therefore favor identifiable payers, acceptance criteria and renewal relationships. In office tools and customer service, the test is whether problems are actually resolved. Knowledge services require attention to licensing and verification costs. Medical documentation volumes cannot substitute for clinical outcomes. Autonomous driving has paid services, but still bears vehicle, maintenance, remote-assistance and utilization risks. Existing channels may help companies retain profit, while implementation and human takeover may cause costs to grow alongside scale. There is no uniform "AI application margin," and revenue at different layers must not be counted repeatedly.

## 5. What I Am Waiting for as an Investor, and What Would Change My Judgment

For investors without a position, I will not endorse high valuations merely because the coding ceiling has been withdrawn or microdrama demand has strengthened. Companies worth prioritizing for research should answer all of the following: Can customers realize the benefits? Can suppliers retain cash after full costs? Can capital investment be recovered? Does the purchase price leave room for one of these outcomes to fall short? Existing holders should put incremental revenue, damage to legacy-business prices and seat counts, debt and dilution into the same model. China's supply chain also requires checks of customers, products, collections and capital spending; "participating in AI" cannot substitute for earnings per share.

The current state remains one of divergence, requiring particular caution about companies whose collections lag while firm payment commitments continue to accumulate. If repeat purchases persist after subsidies are withdrawn, delivery costs fall at equal quality, older equipment continues contributing cash, and operating cash still grows after construction slows, I will acknowledge that time is helping absorb the investment and reduce the weight I place on "delay amplifies risk." Confirming that the forecast industry-wide contraction has occurred would require simultaneous deterioration in orders or collections, lower renewal prices and restricted financing across multiple entities, with traceable transmission, rather than a single stock-price decline or one company's negative cash flow.

This framework also has two particular risks of error. Underestimating tool progress could turn today's delivery friction into an assumed permanent ceiling. Overestimating the speed at which benefits are realized could confuse hours saved, user growth and model calls with shareholder cash. **The first risk makes me willing to raise my assessment of applications; the second makes me maintain my requirements on price and capital recovery.** This brief does not reconstruct valuations for the relevant securities using prices from a common date, and provides neither price targets nor a uniform buy or sell instruction.
