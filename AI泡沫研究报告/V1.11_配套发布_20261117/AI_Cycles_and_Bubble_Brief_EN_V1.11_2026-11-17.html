AI Cycles and the Bubble: Brief

Daduizhang | V1.11 | 2026-11-17 | fqsx@mail.ustc.edu.cn

Core judgments, timing scenarios, AI chip competition and capital recovery

Original research baseline: October 31, 2026 (Beijing time) | Original timing forecast registered: November 1 | Governance, financing and regulatory update: November 17, 2026

I see the first half of 2027 through the first half of 2028 as the main risk window for the current AI capital bubble to burst on a broader scale. Here, a "burst" means sustained valuation repricing across multiple entities, corroborated by pressures on investment, financing and recovery of capital from assets. A single stock-price decline cannot be counted as a successful forecast, nor would a bubble burst mean AI technology has lost its value.

The following is the author's subjective forecast, registered afresh on November 1 using evidence available through October 31, 2026. It is not a date precisely calculated by a model, and does not reinstate the probabilities or forecast declines in earlier editions:

Scenario and possible timing Triggers and transmission Conditions that would change my judgment
Baseline: H1 2027 to H1 2028 Collections lag investment, depreciation on assets entering service and firm payment commitments increase, and downward revisions to returns affect valuations and investment Cash catches up with investment, payback from older assets holds up and pressure stops accumulating
Earlier: November 2026 to Q1 2027 A major customer's payments or refinancing run into trouble first, with procurement, collateral and guarantees spreading the shock Financing and payments recover, without risk spreading across entities
Delayed amplification: H2 2028 to H1 2029 Financing extends expansion, but new firm obligations still outpace collections, leaving more capital unrecovered Construction slows and shortfalls narrow, so the extended cycle begins to help absorb investment
Absorption, avoiding a full-scale bubble burst: observe 2027-2029 Continued payments and efficiency gains absorb earlier investment, while operating cash improves Renewals, cash or financing weaken again, while investment once more runs out of control

If the baseline's repricing across multiple entities and transmission through funding and operations have not appeared by June 30, 2028, that window must be recorded as unfulfilled. It cannot automatically be rolled into the next row and counted as a correct forecast. The four paths are not mutually exclusive and cannot be added into probabilities; the absorption scenario can mean that a full-scale bubble burst is avoided.

Since April, I have consistently argued that "delay amplifies risk." My central judgment remains this: if cash receipts from AI commercialization fail to keep pace with continuing investment, while companies keep locking in the next round of equipment, lease and financing obligations, a prolonged cycle can accumulate greater pressure to recover capital. As GPUs enter service, depreciation also begins to affect profit. Growth in application revenue does not automatically cover both sets of costs.

I also acknowledge that AI has real uses and that commercialization is expanding. Current evidence already shows construction cash shortfalls at some groups, but it does not justify concluding that all AI companies are loss-making or that every project will fail to pay back. My assessment of the current state is divergence among companies, with funding and earnings pressure accumulating at some; an industry-wide simultaneous collapse of demand and financing has not yet occurred.

My investment stance is consequently selective participation: first establish who pays and who retains the profit, then ask whether the purchase price leaves room for error. Application progress deserves more research attention, but it does not automatically justify higher valuations for all models, compute providers and related stocks. This brief retains the original material and sources on microdramas, coding and other applications, and adds the November 17 event research in Section 5. This does not mean all earlier data have been reverified on that date, and it assigns no uncalibrated probability to a crash.

My Core Judgments Before the Commercialization Evidence

Real operating profit, capital circulation and recovery pressure can coexist. I will not use net income that includes investment revaluation as evidence of sustained operations or recovery of new asset costs. Revaluation gains and losses should be adjusted symmetrically, but they do not justify describing real chip, cloud and advertising profits as fake. Cloud providers investing in model companies, model companies buying compute, and equipment vendors providing credit support can enable real delivery. But upstream receipt of cash does not yet establish that continuing payments from customers outside that circle are enough to support the entire chain. Equity investment, credit facilities, procurement and guarantees must be separated by timing and status, rather than added into a single amount already received. Financing can serve as a bridge when customers can renew purchases from operating budgets; if the next round of capital contributions is always needed to pay the previous round's commitments, the cycle remains vulnerable. (Chapters 1 and 2 of the detailed edition)

My assessment of AGI and efficiency gains cuts both ways. Technological leaps may create more billable tasks, or reduce the expensive models needed for the same work and diminish the revenue-generating value of older equipment. Application providers and customers may benefit while investors in older compute capacity and high-cost models may not; better utilization may also extend the revenue-earning lives of older equipment. I no longer use a single AGI year to determine valuations for every company. The question is who ultimately retains the incremental cash. (Chapters 7 and 8)

I do not place technological progress and capital recovery on the same axis. The four cells are assessed for the same business, investment cohort and observation period. "Recovery keeps pace" means ongoing net cash collected from customers can cover the planned recovery of investment and firm payments falling due that have not already been counted. "Recovery lags" means this operating cash is insufficient and additional funding is still needed. The pace of technological progress is assessed through acceptance of newly enabled tasks, reliability and full delivery costs; slower progress does not mean existing capabilities disappear. Cash from legacy businesses and financing provide a buffer, but do not establish payback for new AI assets or justify moving into the left-hand column.

Technological progress / recovery through customer payments Recovery keeps pace, supported by customer cash Recovery lags, requiring additional funding
Continuing technological breakthroughs New tasks expand what can be billed, potentially allowing existing investment to be absorbed. Stronger delivery and distribution provide a buffer; those unable to keep pace with falling costs may still come under pressure. If renewals and gross profit on a cash basis weaken after subsidies are withdrawn, I will revise this assessment downward. Application providers and customers may benefit first, while older equipment, high-cost models and heavily invested projects come under pressure. If lower prices expand usage and net cash from older equipment can also cover investment and payments as planned, I will shift toward an absorption assessment.
Slower technological progress Those with established paying use cases and continuing cash generation have more room to wait; highly valued projects dependent on the next capability leap come under pressure first. If existing-customer renewals or cash from the core business weaken, the buffer assessment no longer holds. Those with concentrated customers, heavy near-term payments and high refinancing dependence are more likely to experience funding pressure first; those with ample cash and the ability to stop expansion have a relative buffer. If payment terms improve and secured funding covers the shortfall, I will lower my assessment of liquidity risk; moving into the left-hand column still requires operating recovery to keep pace.

The four cells describe economic states, while the four paths on the opening page describe timing. Entities can move between cells; a cell cannot be mapped to a single year, added into probabilities or used to predetermine company winners and losers. The cell that warrants the most caution is the one in which technology advances markedly, yet shareholders who bought older assets may receive no incremental cash.

I insist on differentiation rather than predicting the same outcome for platforms, model providers, asset-heavy cloud providers and application companies. Platforms with cash from advertising, software or established cloud businesses have more room to wait, but still face new construction and financing costs. Independent model providers must cover R&D and inference, asset-heavy cloud providers must align lease commencement and debt, and application companies must preserve customer relationships and delivery profit. Entities with concentrated customers, substantial firm payments and heavy refinancing dependence are more likely to come under pressure first. Cash and distribution capabilities provide a relative buffer, but cannot guarantee shareholder returns at any purchase price; upstream "picks and shovels" suppliers are also exposed to customer order cuts. (Chapters 10 and 12)

Opportunities in China must be traced through products and cash, rather than inferred directly from a domestic-substitution label. Similar model capabilities do not imply identical training hardware, engineering ecosystems or energy conditions; chip restrictions do not mean China's AI demand disappears. Overseas CapEx must pass through product mix, supplier shares, prices, delivery and collections before it enters Chinese companies' earnings. Tighter restrictions may promote some substitution but also harm upstream supporting supply; easing may benefit users while depressing local suppliers' prices. I no longer assume that every policy scenario will generate investment returns. (Chapters 8 and 9)

Power, sovereign capital and policy change the conditions for waiting; they do not cancel the bills. Capacity already connected to the grid may benefit from delays to new supply, while projects under construction may tie up more capital. Long-term funding can help projects through construction, or prolong low-return expansion. Actual capital contributions, commissioning and risk sharing must be verified; future energy breakthroughs cannot pay today's rent and debt in advance. The shared lesson from railways, the internet and shale oil is that technological progress, operating payback and investment returns must be verified separately. History cannot supply a fixed decline, amplification multiple or bottom-buying date for this cycle. (Chapter 13 and Appendices C and F)

The evidence below develops these judgments: I am raising my assessment of the potential of microdramas, coding and other applications, while still requiring companies to create benefits for customers and retain cash themselves, with a reasonable purchase price.

1. Why I Still Emphasize "Delay Amplifies Risk"

Three cloud providers offer the clearest comparison. In Microsoft's FY27Q1 ended September 30, operating cash was USD 58 billion. Deducting USD 39.367 billion in cash asset purchases/construction left USD 18.633 billion; after a further USD 1 billion in finance-lease principal, the balance was still USD 17.633 billion. Microsoft results

Alphabet's Cloud business generated revenue of USD 30.709 billion and operating profit of USD 11.121 billion in 2026Q3. Group operating cash for the quarter was USD 42.290 billion, against USD 51.355 billion in cash asset purchases/construction, a difference of negative USD 9.065 billion. Over the trailing twelve months, however, the same measure remained positive at USD 19.747 billion. Alphabet results

Amazon's AWS business generated revenue of USD 45.984 billion and operating profit of USD 17.773 billion in 2026Q3. For the trailing twelve months ended September 30, group operating cash of USD 168.3 billion less net cash asset purchases/construction of USD 210.657 billion produced free cash flow of negative USD 42.357 billion. Amazon results

These periods and definitions cannot be mixed and added together, and cloud segment profit is not the same as returns on the group's AI projects. Together, they show that demand growth is real and that construction absorbs real cash. Evidence of supply-chain shipments, profits and some positive cash generation must also be acknowledged; upstream profits do not establish payback for every downstream project.

My judgment is that capital investment increasingly requires a concrete explanation of how it will be recovered. Construction requires upfront payment for assets used over many years, so a single quarter of negative cash flow does not establish investment failure. But contractual, lease and debt payments do not disappear because the potential future market is large. If investment persistently outpaces sustainable operating cash, new financing will affect interest expense, dilution and returns per share. Continued access to financing shows only that funding has been extended; it cannot substitute for a project's operating payback.

"Current AI revenue cannot cover cash flow" is my shorthand for this recovery pressure. The stricter test begins with customer collections covering necessary operations to generate operating cash, then asks whether that cash can cover construction and payments falling due that are not already included in operating cash. Revenue is not cash, and group cash is not AI-project cash. Existing financial reports support the presence of shortfalls at some groups, but do not support an aggregate industry-wide shortfall attributable specifically to AI. If revenue is delayed while payments are not, or companies commit to new projects before recovering investment in existing ones, waiting will tie up more capital. That is the mechanism behind my judgment; a further period of rising markets alone cannot establish that risk must have increased.

GPU depreciation tests whether earnings can bear the cost of the assets. Costs begin to be allocated once equipment is ready for use. More assets entering service, falling rental prices or insufficient utilization can all make depreciation pressure more pronounced. Older equipment still operating does not mean it can recover its cost at the original price; a new chip generation does not mean older equipment immediately becomes worthless. Rental income, effective utilization, maintenance investment, economic life and residual value should be checked for each equipment batch.

Depreciation is an accounting expense, not another current-period payment for the equipment. Free cash flow already deducts cash asset purchases/construction, so full depreciation cannot be deducted again. A change in accounting useful life changes the allocation of future expenses; impairment requires separate evidence of a trigger. We cannot begin by assuming all GPUs will be scrapped after three years. Longer depreciation periods can temporarily alter the timing of profit, but cannot recover capital already spent. Adding depreciation back in operating cash flow also does not eliminate future equipment replacement spending.

2. Microdramas Bring Video Models Closer to Production Budgets; Competition Will Still Determine Who Captures Profit

I give microdramas greater weight because there is already payment and procurement, not just an audience willing to watch novel videos. Duiba separately reported RMB 222.629 million in AI-microdrama revenue in the first half of 2026, while the group still recorded a loss of RMB 25.326 million over the same period. The former establishes that revenue has occurred; the latter cannot establish a standalone loss or net margin for the microdrama segment. Duiba interim report

Both China and the United States have models combining audience payments and advertising. Free distribution and content procurement in China, and paid apps and cross-border customer acquisition in the United States, operate under different payment and competitive conditions. The overall live-action microdrama market, AI-assisted production, primarily AI-generated works and model-tool revenue must be kept separate. The same finished production can be watched repeatedly, so viewing minutes or audience counts cannot simply be multiplied into a video-generation bill. Time spent in mobile apps also cannot directly establish that films are being displaced across all viewing platforms.

The more meaningful upstream demand from microdramas is studios' continuing production of new dramas, advertising assets, subsequent seasons and localized versions. Customers are not buying unlimited video; they are buying shots that can meet acceptance deadlines and be distributed. Higher acceptance rates and less rework benefit producers, but may reduce generation purchases for the same amount of finished footage. If new genres and new customers expand fast enough, total procurement may increase instead. Which force dominates depends on net paid output, realized prices and full costs.

The three types of vendors also have different paths to profit:

  • Seedance: External model services may generate direct fees, while ByteDance's internal content and advertising businesses may gain efficiency. But internal usage is not new external revenue for the group, and Hongguo's growth does not mean Seedance captures all its procurement. This review did not obtain segment disclosures sufficient to independently recalculate Seedance's revenue, gross profit and training investment.
  • MiniMax: Hailuo, enterprise APIs and open-weight licensing can generate different forms of revenue from professional production. In the first half of 2026, group revenue was USD 116.573 million and gross profit USD 20.813 million, against R&D expense of USD 296.870 million. Existing gross profit was far from covering R&D for the period. The open platform and enterprise services accounted for 63.4% of revenue and included multiple modalities; this cannot all be counted as video or microdrama revenue. Open weights may also broaden adoption while shifting some purchases of hosted services to customers' own compute. MiniMax interim results
  • Kling: Revenue exceeded RMB 850 million in 2026Q2, showing that video tools already generate fees at scale. But the company did not separately disclose complete video profit or microdrama revenue in that release. Kling's external subscriptions and API fees should be accounted for separately from internal advertising and content efficiencies at Kuaishou. Kuaishou Q2 results

I place more weight on the operating ability to deliver repeatedly, control licensed IP and collect payments from users than on "knowing how to use a particular model." Once generation becomes cheaper per second, savings may be consumed by bidding for advertising traffic, unsuccessful productions, rework and platform revenue shares. Once procurement interfaces offer multiple models, underlying suppliers may also lose pricing power. Microdramas expand the potential to charge, but have not resolved how profit will be distributed across the industry.

3. Coding Requires a New Market Model and a New Way to Calculate Savings

The early research multiplied 36.5 million professional developers by a monthly fee of USD 30 and twelve months, producing USD 13.14 billion. This arithmetic is valid under its assumptions, but presenting it as a hard ceiling for the entire market was wrong. V1.5 already withdrew that ceiling; this edition does not present the correction as a new discovery. The original draft also contradicted its "absolute ceiling" with higher-price scenarios and made a unit error by using "USD 13.1 billion" and "$131B" interchangeably.

Usage-based billing, long tasks, code review and nonprofessional uses mean one person's budget is no longer limited to a single low-price seat. GitHub Copilot's move to usage-based billing in June 2026 is concrete product evidence of this change. But annualized revenue, active-user figures and group revenue from different tools cannot simply be combined into a market total free of double counting. This report still has not established that actual revenue from coding alone exceeds the old calculation. GitHub billing explanation

I expect the labor impact to appear first in hiring, departures that are not replaced and standardized outsourcing. A company avoiding layoffs does not mean its planned hiring has not declined; code growth does not mean payroll can fall in the same proportion. The revised Chen-Stratton study of 655 firms found that growth at the generation stage weakened as it passed through to delivery, and detected no significant change in engineer employment at the sampled firms. This is an observational study, and "not detected" cannot be interpreted as ruling out workforce reductions. Study of enterprise organization

Stanford's study using ADP data found that employment among workers aged 22-25 in occupations with high AI exposure lagged the trajectory of peers in low-exposure occupations by 19%, with reduced hiring accounting for much of the divergence. This is not "19% of programmers were laid off," is not an identified net causal effect of AI, and cannot be applied as a labor displacement rate for China. Stanford labor study

The detailed report uses two sets of author assumptions to illustrate budget differences. Neither is a company measurement or an industry average, and both require delivery of equal quality. A ten-person US team releases a net 2,400 hours in steady state, valued at USD 240,000 using internal costs. Deducting USD 40,000 for tools, services and a risk budget leaves net capacity value of USD 200,000. If everyone remains employed, there are no new cash receipts and no purchases are canceled, cash spending and the risk budget instead increase by USD 40,000. A net benefit of USD 140,000 arises only if the company actually avoids a full-year hire costing USD 180,000 and the released capacity covers that person's work.

In the eight-person China example, a net 900 hours are released in steady state, with an internal cost value of RMB 120,000. Deducting RMB 90,000 leaves net capacity value of RMB 30,000. If there are genuinely 900 hours of cancelable outsourcing at RMB 220 per hour, and the internal team can complete equivalent work, the avoided payment is RMB 198,000, leaving a net benefit of RMB 108,000 after deducting RMB 90,000. Net capacity value cannot be added to the benefit from this alternative use of the same capacity.

The USD 40,000 and RMB 90,000 both include risk budgets, so they do not consist entirely of cash already paid. Training, integration and parallel validation also occur in the first year, so steady-state benefits cannot be applied directly. Lower-wage teams can benefit, but they have a greater need to control review and tool costs or find spending that can actually be canceled. Of every RMB 100 a customer saves, only a portion will eventually become supplier revenue, and competition will determine its allocation.

4. Other Applications Increase the Importance I Place on Existing Payment Relationships

AI commercialization extends beyond coding and video. AlphaSense disclosed company ARR above USD 700 million in 2026Q2, supporting the presence of paid scale in knowledge workflows. This is a company-reported annualized recurring metric that includes the value of content, search and the platform; it is not quarterly revenue or incremental generative-AI revenue. AlphaSense original disclosure

Duolingo generated USD 78.63 million in free cash flow under its own definition in 2026Q2. This establishes that an education product containing AI features can generate positive cash, but does not allow all course revenue or profit to be attributed to AI. Duolingo Q2 results

In contrast, Character.ai entered a court-supervised sale process in October despite already having subscription and advertising revenue. The service was still operating at the time; it must not be described as already shut down or sold. This shows that user value, monetization and shareholder returns can diverge, but does not invalidate all companion products. Character.ai official announcement

My research priorities therefore favor identifiable payers, acceptance criteria and renewal relationships. In office tools and customer service, the test is whether problems are actually resolved. Knowledge services require attention to licensing and verification costs. Medical documentation volumes cannot substitute for clinical outcomes. Autonomous driving has paid services, but still bears vehicle, maintenance, remote-assistance and utilization risks. Existing channels may help companies retain profit, while implementation and human takeover may cause costs to grow alongside scale. There is no uniform "AI application margin," and revenue at different layers must not be counted repeatedly.

5. November 17 Update: How Huang's Departure Affects Financing and Capital Recovery

My main window remains H1 2027 through H1 2028. This event raises the monitoring priority of the earlier-credit-shock path, but has not established a financing halt or an industry-wide bubble burst. The SEC filing confirms that the board accepted Huang's resignation as president, CEO and a director on November 11, with the announcement on November 12; Kress serves as interim CEO while remaining CFO, and Jones becomes chairman. The company says operations and the roadmap continue; actual delivery remains to be tested. SEC leadership 8-K

Huang's importance lies in long-term technological choices and his ability to coordinate customers, suppliers and financial institutions. Existing CUDA assets, systems and the R&D organization do not disappear on the day of his departure, although new projects that are unsigned or have not closed may be reassessed. A new CEO's financial background does not necessarily imply cuts to R&D or customer support; stronger capital discipline could slow short-term growth while reducing future losses.

Financing relationships must be accounted for separately: the USD 2 billion CoreWeave equity investment has already been made; the initial USD 6.3 billion order for unsold capacity is subject to delivery conditions; the SB Energy/OpenAI guarantee has an aggregate cap of USD 105 billion and takes effect in phases; and the financial-institution platforms' USD 500 billion figure is a long-term third-party capital target. These cannot be added into a total of cash received, and existing contracts do not automatically disappear when the CEO changes. NVIDIA's cloud services for its own R&D serve a different purpose from support for customers. (Original contractual wording and funding status are in Sections 14.3–14.4 of the detailed edition.)

Supplier profits can coexist with a customer's construction cash shortfall. CoreWeave's operating cash for the first nine months was USD 5.638 billion, against USD 23.558 billion in cash purchases/construction of equipment and related assets, a difference of negative USD 17.920 billion; this is not negative operating cash flow. Net financing cash of USD 21.346 billion over the same period had already provided a buffer, so the difference cannot be equated with a funding shortfall that remained unfilled, nor does that financing establish operating payback. September-end liquidity of USD 9.837 billion included USD 5.653 billion of available credit and was not entirely cash. The transfer of USD 1.9 billion of opening deferred revenue to customer liabilities because the associated contracts became cancelable also does not mean refunds had already occurred. The relevant operating periods preceded the departure and cannot be attributed to the leadership event. CoreWeave Q3 10-Q

The purely hypothetical project in Chapter 14 illustrates both sides. Delayed acceptance, capital already tied up in equipment and continuing interest put pressure on cash; simultaneous price and utilization declines weaken recovery further. But deliberately slowing expansion can reduce funding pressure if payment terms are actually renegotiated. Paid-in replacement equity can ease liquidity without changing the project's own operating recovery, and may dilute existing shareholders. A path with a cash shortfall requires funding to be filled before it can be executed; a table with negative cash cannot be used to pretend construction can continue indefinitely, and depreciation cannot be deducted as a second cash payment.

BIS announced its remote-compute rule on November 16, with publication scheduled for November 19; it is not yet effective as of the research date. Equipment destinations and ultimate cloud users require separate examination, and licenses, exceptions and transitions cannot be omitted. This is not a ban on all generative APIs, microdramas or coding applications serving Chinese users. China's share of direct revenue also cannot measure ultimate end-use exposure: geographic revenue is classified by the headquarters of direct customers, while ultimate users and data-center locations may differ. Federal Register public inspection notice

I therefore see two paths. If new financing, approvals and delivery are delayed while fixed payments continue, pressure may spread across companies. If succession is stable, funding is actually contributed and low-return commitments fall, risk may instead decline. The November 16 report that partner financial institutions were still discussing projects must also be retained as counterevidence; I cannot collect only material that supports my own judgment. Reuters

Next I will examine formal succession and financial disclosures, project acceptance and drawdowns, and collections and net firm obligations in subsequent quarters. NVIDIA's FY27Q3 results are due only at approximately 05:20 on November 18 Beijing time; this report does not use them in advance. Only sustained repricing across multiple entities, accompanied by transmission through funding and capital recovery from assets, would meet the original definition of a burst. The core judgments, two matrices and June 30, 2028 accountability deadline remain unchanged. The expansion of use cases merits recognition; capital recovery still requires separate verification.

6. What I Am Waiting for as an Investor, and What Would Change My Judgment

For investors without a position, I will not endorse high valuations merely because the coding ceiling has been withdrawn or microdrama demand has strengthened. Companies worth prioritizing for research should answer all of the following: Can customers realize the benefits? Can suppliers retain cash after full costs? Can capital investment be recovered? Does the purchase price leave room for one of these outcomes to fall short? Existing holders should put incremental revenue, damage to legacy-business prices and seat counts, debt and dilution into the same model. China's supply chain also requires checks of customers, products, collections and capital spending; "participating in AI" cannot substitute for earnings per share.

The current state remains one of divergence, requiring particular caution about companies whose collections lag while firm payment commitments continue to accumulate. If repeat purchases persist after subsidies are withdrawn, delivery costs fall at equal quality, older equipment continues contributing cash, and operating cash still grows after construction slows, I will acknowledge that time is helping absorb the investment and reduce the weight I place on "delay amplifies risk." Confirming that the forecast industry-wide contraction has occurred would require simultaneous deterioration in orders or collections, lower renewal prices and restricted financing across multiple entities, with traceable transmission, rather than a single stock-price decline or one company's negative cash flow.

This framework also has two particular risks of error. Underestimating tool progress could turn today's delivery friction into an assumed permanent ceiling. Overestimating the speed at which benefits are realized could confuse hours saved, user growth and model calls with shareholder cash. The first risk makes me willing to raise my assessment of applications; the second makes me maintain my requirements on price and capital recovery. This brief does not reconstruct valuations for the relevant securities using prices from a common date, and provides neither price targets nor a uniform buy or sell instruction.

7. Competition Update: AMD and TPUs Change Who Bears Risk

I am raising my assessment of second suppliers' practical capabilities, but do not equate NVIDIA-specific risk with industry demand risk. If customer budgets and funding remain intact, AMD, TPUs and cloud providers' own chips can take on some tasks. If common payers cut budgets, changing chips will not create new cash. The previous edition already mentioned TPU system sales, AMD warrants and Broadcom notes. This update completes the wider mechanism; agreements predating November 11 cannot be attributed to Huang's departure. Customer arrangements with multiple suppliers

Substitution now has evidence from actual hardware, alongside plans that remain in the future. Customers are running AMD's initial Helios racks, and Meta has published production research on MI350X recommendation-model training. That case cannot establish universal superiority across large models or at equal power and cost. Google Ironwood is generally available, while the eighth-generation family remained Coming soon on the November 17 product page. AWS Trn3 is generally available; Maia200 already has production workloads, while Maia300 awaits 2027. Production use, rental availability and deployment of an entire tranche must be confirmed separately. Vendor benchmarks cannot be compared across different tasks. Meta, TPU product page, Trn3, Maia

What customers actually compare is full task cost at equal quality and SLA, including software migration, networking, power and reruns after failures. AMD may gain share, or simply help customers lower NVIDIA's quote. When the total market shrinks and support costs rise, increasing share and falling profit can coexist. TPU internal cost reductions, Cloud fees and system sales are separate accounts. Alphabet has not disclosed standalone TPU product profit; total cloud profit cannot substitute for it. Software migration requirements, Alphabet Q3 discussion

Competitors do more than collect equipment payments. At the end of September, AMD already had external compute commitments and lease guarantees. Some TPUs sold externally by Alphabet carry repurchase support, while Broadcom participates in equipment residual-value support and financing for customer rent. Contractual commitments, facilities under which notes have not yet been issued, and obligations triggered by default are not current cash shortfalls, and cannot be added to the corresponding equipment transaction values. Different chips may still serve the same model company; aggregate GW agreements and cloud projects within them must be deduplicated. Technological diversity therefore does not establish diversification of ultimate credit risk. AMD, Alphabet, Broadcom

The strongest counterevidence to a pessimistic judgment is not another performance number from a challenger. It is customers continuing to buy at lower full cost, older equipment finding suitable workloads, and cash covering payments. If that evidence persists, I should acknowledge that competition has improved industry resilience. Conversely, if support in which suppliers provide funds and customers buy equipment keeps expanding without more net collections from ultimate users, a longer customer list does not establish lower risk. Selling systems externally also changes who holds the assets, without necessarily reducing the industry's total investment still to be recovered. System sales and risk-bearing

I retain two opposing paths. If lower costs expand paid tasks while utilization and net cash from older equipment hold up, competition helps absorb investment. If new platforms depress older GPUs' rental prices while duplicate construction and financing obligations keep growing, recovery of older capital instead deteriorates. Shared fabrication, packaging, memory, power and licensing also prevent instantaneous substitution. The next checks are actual acceptance first, then transaction prices, renewals and net collections, followed by drawdowns, firm payments and guarantees. Only sustained deterioration across multiple entities, with transmission between them, warrants escalating the industry judgment. These are conditional inferences, without new probabilities or price targets. The original main window and accountability for an unfulfilled forecast on June 30, 2028 remain unchanged.
