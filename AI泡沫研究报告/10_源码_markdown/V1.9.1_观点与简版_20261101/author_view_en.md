# Author's View: Delay Amplifies Risk Unless Cash Recovery Catches Up with Investment

Research cutoff: October 31, 2026 (Beijing time) | Edited: November 1, 2026

**I see the first half of 2027 through the first half of 2028 as the main risk window for the current AI capital bubble to burst on a broader scale.** Here, a "burst" means sustained valuation repricing across multiple entities, corroborated by pressures on investment, financing and recovery of capital from assets. A single stock-price decline does not satisfy that definition, nor would a bubble burst mean AI technology has lost its value.

This is the author's subjective forecast, registered afresh on November 1 using evidence available through October 31, 2026. It is not a date precisely calculated by a data model, and carries no uncalibrated probability or forecast decline.

| Scenario and possible timing | Triggers and transmission I anticipate | What would change my judgment |
|---|---|---|
| **Baseline: H1 2027 to H1 2028** | Collections continue to lag; depreciation on assets entering service and firm payment commitments increase, with downward revisions to expected returns affecting valuations and investment | Revenue and cash catch up with investment, while older assets continue earning sustainable returns |
| Earlier credit shock: November 2026 to Q1 2027 | A major customer's payments or refinancing are disrupted, with pressure spreading through procurement, collateral and guarantees | Long-term funding is secured and payments resume, without the shock spreading across entities |
| Delayed amplification: H2 2028 to H1 2029 | Financing continues to support expansion while new firm obligations still outpace collections, ultimately creating greater capital-recovery pressure | Expansion slows and shortfalls narrow, so the extended cycle allows investment to be absorbed rather than pressure to accumulate |
| Absorption, avoiding a full-scale bubble burst: observe 2027-2029 | Payments and efficiency gains absorb earlier investment, construction slows in an orderly way and cash improves | Net cash persistently lags, while renewals and financing deteriorate again |

These are four possible paths, not probabilities that can be added together. **If the baseline's repricing across multiple entities and transmission through funding and operations have not appeared by June 30, 2028, I will record that window as unfulfilled. I cannot automatically extend it and count the forecast as correct.**

**Since April, I have consistently argued that "delay amplifies risk": when cash receipts from AI commercialization fail to keep pace with continuing investment, while companies take on further equipment, lease and financing commitments, a prolonged cycle can leave ever more capital unrecovered and funding gaps wider.** Some groups already face construction cash shortfalls, and growth in application revenue has not automatically removed this risk. This remains my central risk judgment, even as I acknowledge expanding real demand. My assessment of the current state is divergence among companies and localized accumulation of capital-recovery pressure; an industry-wide simultaneous collapse of demand and financing has not yet occurred.

**1. I do not accept "AI has no real demand" as a reason to be bearish.** Cloud services, supply-chain companies, video tools and enterprise applications already have verifiable revenue, and some companies also generate operating profit and cash. Microdramas and coding deserve particular attention: they allow customers to pay for specific content and work that can be assessed against acceptance criteria. Describing today's market in terms of its early demonstration phase would miss changes in commercialization. But a supplier receiving payment does not mean every customer buying equipment and models has recovered its investment.

**2. I am more concerned about the pace of capital recovery than the reality of revenue.** Microsoft still had a cash surplus after construction spending in its latest fiscal quarter; Alphabet had a quarterly shortfall but remained positive over the trailing year; Amazon's construction spending exceeded operating cash over the trailing year. These three cases cannot be combined into a conclusion that "the industry's cash has already run dry." My concern is that future construction, lease and financing payments will fall due under contracts, while revenue growth may not turn into available cash in time. Negative cash flow during construction is not itself failure; persistent dependence on new money to meet existing commitments is what increases risk.

**GPU depreciation is the other half of this judgment.** Once equipment enters service, its cost is gradually recognized in profit. If rental prices, utilization and economic lives fall short of expectations, revenue growth may not offset the pressure from expenses. Depreciation is not another cash payment in the current period, and free cash flow that already deducts cash equipment purchases must not deduct the same cost again. Shorter useful lives and asset impairments also require separate verification; we cannot assume that every GPU must be scrapped after three years. Group cash does not establish payback for each batch of AI assets.

**3. I expect microdramas to expand video models' opportunities to charge, but I do not expect the three vendors to benefit in equal proportion.** Seedance can create value through external customers buying its model services or through more efficient content and advertising within ByteDance. MiniMax has a greater need for Hailuo and its multimodal services to cover inference and R&D costs. Kling already has direct revenue, but its standalone profit still needs to be established. Finished productions, advertising assets and localization can generate repeat purchases; less rework may also reduce the generated seconds purchased for the same amount of finished footage. Net prices, acceptance rates, repeat purchases, inference costs and control of distribution determine profit, rather than view counts themselves.

**4. I expect coding tools to change hiring and outsourcing budgets first, then gradually change existing headcount.** Junior implementation work, repetitive testing and standardized outsourcing are more exposed to pressure; review, integration, responsibility and new demand will limit job cuts. Hours saved on tasks can allow the existing team to do more, or allow a company to avoid hiring. Those are different economic outcomes. Cash benefits are realized only when payroll, outsourcing or other payments actually fall, or attributable contribution cash increases. If everyone remains employed and no new cash receipts materialize, tool fees may initially increase spending.

**5. I prefer to research applications with established routes to payment and an advantage in delivery.** Enterprise workflows, licensed knowledge, established subscriptions, and medical and physical services in defined settings have clearer routes from capability to existing budgets. This is a research priority, however, not a list of winners. Distribution, customer relationships, content rights and reliable delivery may help companies retain profits; falling model prices, bundled suites, human review and customer acquisition may consume them. A product with revenue may still be unable to sustain its business, while a lack of separately disclosed AI profit does not establish that it is loss-making.

**6. I will not raise the price I am willing to pay merely because the market is growing.** For investors without a position, I prioritize companies whose customers can realize benefits, that can retain cash from supplying those customers, and whose share prices have not already priced in success. For existing holders, I focus more on cash per share, debt and dilution than on repeatedly confirming enthusiasm for the industry. Without prices from a common valuation date and a complete valuation model, this report gives no price targets and does not present an industry judgment as a trading instruction.

I must explicitly take responsibility for the most important revision to my forecasts: the early approach of multiplying the number of professional developers by a fixed low monthly fee, then treating the result as a hard ceiling for the entire coding market, was wrong. **V1.5 already withdrew that ceiling; this edition provides further explanation and verification, rather than presenting the correction as a new discovery.** Usage-based billing, long tasks and nonprofessional uses expand the scope of what can be billed. That still does not establish that actual market revenue, after removing double counting, exceeds the old calculation, or that any tool's valuation is reasonable.

What would change my judgment? If customers continue paying after subsidies decline, full delivery costs fall, older equipment still contributes cash, and revenue and cash continue growing as construction slows, I will acknowledge that time is helping absorb the investment and reduce the weight I place on "delay amplifies risk." Conversely, if several comparable companies simultaneously experience weaker collections, lower renewal prices and restricted financing, with observable transmission of risk between them, I will treat that as evidence that the forecast industry-wide contraction is materializing. **I am raising my assessment of application potential while continuing to test capital recovery. The two positions are consistent.**
