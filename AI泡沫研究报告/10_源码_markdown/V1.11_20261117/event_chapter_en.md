## Chapter 14: Huang's Departure, AI Financing and Capital Recovery

This chapter is an event update dated November 17, 2026. The original baseline for Chapters 1–13 and the application research is October 31. This chapter reverifies governance, related financial and financing matters, and regulatory material; it does not mean all earlier data on microdramas, coding and other applications have been rechecked as of November 17. Amounts are in US dollars unless otherwise specified.

### 14.1 My Judgment: More Reason for Earlier Tests, but No Change to the Main Window Yet

**I still place the main risk window for a broader bursting of the AI capital bubble in H1 2027 through H1 2028. Huang's departure adds a potential trigger for capital-recovery pressure to emerge earlier, but is not yet sufficient to move this subjective window forward.** The previously registered "credit shock from November 2026 to Q1 2027" path now warrants more immediate examination. Higher monitoring priority does not mean financing deterioration has already been measured, much less that the arrival of the news itself makes the forecast correct.

The SEC filing confirms that the board accepted Huang's resignation as president, CEO and a director on November 11 US local time, effective immediately; the announcement followed only on November 12. Colette Kress remains CFO and also serves as interim CEO, Harvey C. Jones becomes chairman, and the company is seeking a permanent successor from internal and external candidates. The board says operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap will continue; this is a company statement that must be tested against operating outcomes. [Leadership 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000082/nvda-20261111.htm), [transition announcement](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition)

An independent investigation had begun on November 8, and the engagement of outside counsel was announced on November 10. The existence of an investigation does not mean allegations have been judicially established. Even if subsequent findings confirm management misconduct, that would not directly establish financial-statement fraud. This chapter analyzes only governance, product delivery, customer eligibility and capital commitments. [Board statement](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors), [independent directors' statement](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-the-independent-members-of-the-nvidia-board)

The event's economic significance lies in NVIDIA's dual role: it supplies a computing platform and also participates in downstream expansion through equity, procurement support and guarantees. A change of CEO at a technology supplier may have a different reach from a change at a supplier that also coordinates financing for large projects. But moving from "could" to "has" requires evidence of changes in contracts, drawdowns, delivery and collections.

### 14.2 An Important Coordinator Is Lost; Technology and the Organization Still Need Separate Assessment

I believe the hardest part of Huang's role to replace is bringing long-term technological choices, customer demand, supply chains and capital commitments into the same set of decisions. A next-generation platform often requires chips, networking, software and data-center conditions to be ready together. Major customers also need confidence that supply will continue, while financial institutions need confidence that assets will find users. A founder's judgment and reputation can reduce the cost of different institutions waiting for one another's confirmation. This role particularly warrants reassessment for new projects that are unsigned, have not closed or still await approval.

But it cannot be converted into an unverified "share of revenue attributable to one person." CUDA code, software libraries, GPU and CPU architectures, networking, testing processes and customer deployments are organizational assets. The Rubin technical description describes system-level coordination and CUDA backward compatibility. From this, I infer that a CEO's departure does not stop existing training, inference and applications on the same day, or automatically give competitors an equivalent ecosystem. Compatibility still requires customer testing, and vendors' performance multiples cannot substitute for delivery results on real workloads. [Rubin platform technical description](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/inside-the-nvidia-rubin-platform-six-new-chips-one-ai-supercomputer/)

Specific executives provide organizational continuity. The company website still lists Debora Shoquist as responsible for manufacturing, foundries, suppliers, logistics and quality, and Chris Malachowsky remains a member of the executive team and a senior technology executive. Nick Parker had already taken over sales operations in August: a July SEC filing had disclosed Puri's retirement transition, and the website confirms Parker joined in August. This earlier arrangement must not be described as staff departures following the November crisis, nor should an older annual report be used to continue treating Puri as the current sales leader. [Operations executive biography](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/bios/debora-shoquist), [technology executive biography](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/bios/chris-a-malachowsky), [sales transition 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000060/nvda-20260628.htm), [Parker biography](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/bios/nick-parker)

This leaves both risk and a buffer. With sales leadership having just changed and the CEO now departing, coordination across teams must be reverified. The new sales leader also has customer and channel experience at large technology companies, which may strengthen institutional management. The real tests are product milestones, quality, customer acceptance, staff stability and conversion into new contracts, rather than simply whether the new CEO resembles the founder.

Governance changes must also be assessed against the actual baseline. The May proxy already classified all directors other than Huang as independent under Nasdaq standards and designated Stephen Neal as independent Lead Director. Jones has been on the board since 1993. A new chairman does not mean the company has independent oversight for the first time, or that governance has improved comprehensively. Improvement requires subsequent actions: how major customer support is authorized, how conflicts of interest are disclosed, whether investments, sales and guarantees are reviewed separately, and how adverse information reaches the board. [2026 proxy, director independence and leadership structure](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000036/nvda-20260512.htm)

Kress's combined CEO and CFO roles may give capital review greater weight, while also increasing her workload. Her financial background does not prove R&D will necessarily contract; stronger capital discipline is not necessarily harmful either. Reducing new commitments with low expected returns may depress short-term growth while improving future cash and the company's risk profile.

### 14.3 Separate Five Types of Funding Relationships Before Discussing a Break in the Financing Chain

**Equity, procurement, guarantees, unpaid commitments and third-party capital targets do not belong in the same accounting bucket.** They may surround the same project, but differ in loss priority, payment conditions and maturity. Adding headline amounts together double counts the same economic relationships and mistakes future ceilings for cash today.

| Arrangement | Verified amount and contractual status | What this event principally tests |
|---|---|---|
| CoreWeave equity | USD 2 billion cash investment completed on January 23, 2026; announced January 26 | The completed investment bears equity-value risk; any additional investment requires a separate decision |
| Procurement of unsold CoreWeave capacity | September 2025 order with an initial value of USD 6.3 billion, running through April 2032; remaining capacity is purchased after conditions including service delivery and availability are satisfied | Initial order value is not the current unpaid balance, or an unconditional backstop for all revenue |
| SB Energy/OpenAI construction guarantee | Signed August 17, 2026, with an aggregate cap of USD 105 billion and approximately 4.25 GW; takes effect in phases, with the first expected in FY2029 | Distant contingent obligations may affect credit judgments, but cannot all be put into the next quarter's cash shortfall |
| Financing platforms with six financial institutions | August memoranda of understanding targeting the long-term mobilization of more than USD 500 billion of third-party capital; final arrangements still pending | Independent underwriting and project-by-project execution; NVIDIA may provide limited residual-value support at its discretion, rather than guaranteeing the entire target |

A CEO's departure does not itself terminate corporate contracts. Each agreement's triggers, termination, delivery and default provisions must be examined. The public disclosures reviewed for this update provide no evidence of "automatic termination upon Huang's departure," but some agreements are redacted, so this cannot guarantee that no terms contain key-person conditions. The most immediate candidates for review are new support not yet committed, discretionary extensions and unfinished financings. [CoreWeave/NVIDIA service agreement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000114036125036118/ny20053122x6_ex10-31.htm)

Moreover, more direct customers do not necessarily mean diversified ultimate demand. NVIDIA's Q2 disclosure states that an unnamed AI research and deployment company contributed a meaningful amount of revenue by purchasing cloud services through NVIDIA's customers. No company name or quantifiable share is provided. This reminds me that multiple integrators, cloud providers and financing projects may still serve the same ultimate demand. Investment, shipments, offtake and guarantees cannot each be counted as four independent sources of demand. [FY27Q2 10-Q, indirect customer disclosure](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm)

The USD 29 billion of R&D cloud services is for the company's own R&D and cannot be described as customer rescue funding; the USD 36 billion of customer support does carry real economic costs. Data centers intended for transfer to third parties do not mean the obligations have already been transferred. Some supply commitments can be adjusted before firm orders, potentially at a cost; different projects cannot all be treated as wholly irrevocable. [Q2 10-Q, Notes 7 and 10](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm)

My interpretation of the timing is that supply payments, collection of receivables and fulfillment of equity commitments require the first checks; more customer cloud support and leases reach their payment periods later, while SB Energy has an even more distant phased commencement. A guarantee cap can affect willingness to finance today, but is not a cash loss already incurred today. Period-end balances and agreements signed after period end also cannot be combined as if they existed at the same date.

NVIDIA generated USD 24.077 billion of operating cash in FY27Q2 ended July 26, and USD 21.341 billion of free cash flow under its own definition, still demonstrating strong cash generation. Meanwhile, changes in receivables absorbed USD 22.346 billion of cash during the quarter. The company disclosed that DSO increased from 45 to 60 days, explaining this as extended payment terms on large, multiquarter contracts with some investment-grade customers. This indicates slower cash realization, but does not automatically establish overdue balances or bad debts. Subsequent collections, aging and loss allowances need examination. [NVIDIA Q2 cash-flow statement](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-second-quarter-fiscal-2027/), [CFO commentary](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000073/q2fy27cfocommentary.htm)

Company-defined FCF does not deduct all equity investments and shareholder distributions. During the same quarter, cash purchases of equity securities were USD 15.822 billion, repurchases and dividends were USD 25.779 billion, and net proceeds from debt issuance were USD 24.896 billion. This does not mean the company needs a rescue; it shows that uses of funds span operating, investing and financing categories, so FCF cannot be treated as the freely available balance after every expenditure. [Q2 cash-flow statement](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-second-quarter-fiscal-2027/)

Connections therefore extend further beyond the sale: longer payment terms make the supplier wait for cash; equity exposes it to customer valuation; offtake makes it bear some risk of absorbing capacity; and guarantees may require it to assume payments or leases under specified conditions. The risk is not the same dollar circulating endlessly, but a redistribution of different levels of loss exposure within projects. If ultimate customers keep paying, such support can serve as a bridge. If each new construction phase depends on the next round of support, high supplier profits still do not establish payback for downstream assets.

### 14.5 CoreWeave's New Results: Real Growth Coexists with the Need for External Funding

The Q3 results released on November 10 are new evidence relative to the October 31 baseline. CoreWeave's operating cash inflow for the first nine months was USD 5.638 billion, against USD 23.558 billion in cash purchases/construction of property and equipment and capitalized internal-use software, a difference of negative USD 17.920 billion. For Q3 alone, USD 1.975 billion less USD 9.441 billion equals negative USD 7.466 billion. Depreciation has not been deducted twice here, and capital expenditure guidance has not been substituted for cash payments. [Q3 announcement](https://investors.coreweave.com/news/news-details/2026/CoreWeave-Reports-Strong-Third-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx), [Q3 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000465/crwv-20260930.htm)

In the same quarter, revenue was USD 3.661 billion and GAAP operating profit USD 207 million, but net interest expense was USD 846 million and the net loss USD 709 million. This shows that genuine customer demand and operating improvement can coexist with insufficient coverage of financing and other expenses. These periods ended September 30 and cannot be attributed to the November departure. Net interest on the income statement is also not the same as cash interest paid in that quarter and cannot be deducted again in full from operating cash flow.

September 30 liquidity must be separated into its components: USD 4.122 billion of cash and cash equivalents, USD 62 million of marketable securities, and USD 5.653 billion available under existing credit facilities, totaling USD 9.837 billion. The last category includes facilities subject to drawdown conditions, so the total is not all cash. A further USD 2.282 billion of restricted cash cannot be assumed freely available to pay all the group's bills. Net financing cash of USD 21.346 billion already received in the first nine months is a historical cash bridge and cannot be added again. The company's expectation of approximately USD 9 billion in customer prepayments and deposits over the following three months is a forecast, not cash received by that date. [Q3 10-Q, cash flows, Note 10 and Liquidity](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000465/crwv-20260930.htm)

Changes in terms are more revealing. In Q3, USD 1.9 billion of opening deferred revenue was transferred to customer liabilities because the associated contracts became cancelable. **This does not mean customers have canceled or refunds have occurred, but it removes the basis for assuming that prepayment permanently locks in funding.** Further checks must establish cancellation, refund rights and whether corresponding construction obligations can be released. The company also disclosed compliance with loan covenants as of September 30; the cash shortfall should not be presented while omitting these buffers. [Q3 10-Q, Notes 2 and 10](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000465/crwv-20260930.htm)

Loan and customer contract terms are not inherently aligned either. The August DDTL 5.5 announcement described an approximately five-year loan supported by underlying customer contracts averaging approximately three years. Lenders' willingness to bear some renewal risk expands financing capacity; rental prices, renewals and residual values after customer contracts expire consequently enter the repayment logic. "An average of three years" cannot be described as the term of every contract, nor can a different set of higher-priced three- to six-month contracts be used as proof that this loan already has long-term coverage. [Loan announcement](https://investors.coreweave.com/news/news-details/2026/CoreWeave-Closes-2-6-Billion-Loan-Facility-Expanding-Financing-Flexibility-for-AI-Infrastructure/default.aspx), [short-term contract announcement](https://investors.coreweave.com/news/news-details/2026/CoreWeave-Continues-to-Contract-New-Compute-Capacity-at-Higher-Prices/default.aspx)

### 14.6 A Purely Hypothetical Project: When Delay Amplifies Risk and When Slowing Down Helps

Everything below is an analytical illustration by the author, not a forecast for NVIDIA, CoreWeave or any real project. Units change to USD millions. Each period is one full settlement month, with the first period labeled M0 and charged a full month's costs and interest. Assume an initial project investment of 100: 40 is paid in period 1 (M0), 40 in period 2 (M1), and 20 upon acceptance. Acceptance and collections begin in period 5 (M4). Monthly revenue at full utilization is 8, or 6 at 75% utilization. Variable payments are 1.5, fixed operating payments 1.5 and maintenance CapEx 0.2, leaving normal monthly net cash before debt service of 2.8. Preparation costs before acceptance are 0.3 per month.

Paid-in equity of 45 and an actual loan drawdown of 60 arrive in period 1. Annual interest is 10%, calculated on principal after the drawdown at the start of the month and before that period's principal repayment. The first principal-repayment index is the drawdown index plus 13, which in the baseline is period 14 (M13); principal of 1 is then repaid in each period, for 60 repayments in total. Undrawn facilities are not treated as cash. Simple project payback means the first time cumulative pretax, undiscounted net project cash becomes nonnegative. It accumulates only customer cash less operating payments and capital investment, excluding financing inflows, interest and principal repayments; it is neither net present value nor shareholder return. The project cash balance including financing separately records paid-in equity, drawdowns, interest and principal repayments, avoiding counting both equipment cost and financing principal as project costs. This balance is not cash already distributed to shareholders.

> Month-end cash = previous month's cash + paid-in equity + actual drawdowns + customer cash − operating payments − maintenance CapEx − initial project payments − cash interest − principal repayments.

| Purely hypothetical case | Maximum additional funding needed in first 25 periods | Cash at end of period 13 | Cash at end of period 25 | Simple project payback |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Baseline | 0.00 | 22.50 | 38.65 | Period 41 (M40) |
| Drawdown delayed 3 months, original construction schedule maintained | 35.90 | 24.00* | 42.90* | Period 41 (M40)* |
| Acceptance delayed 6 months, with final payment and start of operations deferred | 0.70 | 3.90 | 20.05 | Period 47 (M46) |
| The same acceptance delay, plus a 20% price cut and 60% utilization | 7.85 | −1.68 | −7.85 | Still unrecovered in period 73 (M72) |
| Deliberate slowdown of 6 months, with negotiated deferral of payments and drawdown | 0.00 | 8.90 | 31.58 | Period 47 (M46) |
| Original loan unavailable; replacement equity of 60 paid in during period 1 | 0.00 | 29.00 | 62.60 | Period 41 (M40) |

The table's maximum additional funding need covers only periods 1–25, or M0–M24 in the model. It is the absolute value of the most negative month-end cash balance, with a floor of zero. The stressed case's 7.85 is not a ceiling on funding needs over the project's entire life: if the original path continues without additional funding, the minimum balance across the full 73-period observation is approximately −20.53, still requiring further funding, adjusted payments or termination of the original path. **Negative cash means supplementary funding must first be found for the assumed construction and operations to proceed; it does not mean unlimited overdrafts are possible in reality.** The delayed-drawdown row isolates only the timing of financing. Its temporary liquidity need of 35.90 must be filled first for the original schedule and period-41 payback to hold. Unknown bridge-financing costs are excluded. Slightly higher cash later comes from deferring interest and principal repayments, rather than a free benefit from delay. If funding cannot be found, the scenario must instead shift to delayed construction or acceptance.

The delayed-acceptance case does not hold all payments fixed either. The final payment of 20, normal operating expenses and maintenance investment move with acceptance. The earlier 80 has already been paid, while preparation costs and interest on drawn debt continue. A six-month delay reduces period-13 ending cash from 22.50 to 3.90. The difference of 18.60 reflects fewer operating months and waiting costs; the equipment payment of 100 cannot be counted again as another loss.

Price and utilization can further impair recovery capacity. With a 20% price cut and 60% utilization, revenue is 8 × 0.8 × 0.6 = 3.84. Variable payments decline to 1.2; after fixed payments of 1.5 and maintenance of 0.2, only 0.94 remains before debt service, 1.86 below the baseline. The equipment still generates a positive operating contribution, but this is insufficient to cover approximately 1 of principal and 0.5 of interest initially. Project investment remains unrecovered as of period 73 (M72).

The slowdown counterexample requires counterparties actually to agree to changed terms: the installation payment of 40 moves from period 2 (M1) to period 8 (M7); the loan is not drawn until period 8, with the first principal repayment deferred accordingly; preparation costs fall to 0.15 per month; and there are no rescheduling penalties. Acceptance still occurs in period 11 (M10) and payback in period 47 (M46), but the cash trough improves. In reality, rent that cannot be deferred, equipment already delivered or penalties would weaken the effect. A "deliberate slowdown" must be implemented through cancelable investment or changed payment terms; announcing lower spending does not automatically confer those rights.

Replacement equity also solves only part of the problem. Investors' paid-in capital rises from 45 to 105 and the loan of 60 is removed, improving liquidity and the interest burden; the project itself still pays back in period 41 (M40). New shareholders require returns and existing shareholders may be diluted. New capital cannot be treated as operating revenue, nor does financing arriving establish that the assets are more profitable.

Separately assume the original equipment cost of 100 is depreciated straight-line over 72 months with no residual value, giving monthly depreciation of approximately 1.389. Simplified baseline EBIT is 1.611; in the lower-price, lower-utilization case it is approximately −0.249. This illustrates only depreciation of the original equipment and does not model subsequent depreciation of maintenance assets. Depreciation is not deducted again in the cash table. The 72-month depreciation period is a separate, arbitrary illustrative input, not a conclusion about GPU lives or a mistaken count of M0–M72 as 72 periods. The model also omits taxes, bad debts, intramonth peaks, rescheduling penalties, bridge-financing costs, discounting and terminal value. It cannot be multiplied by industry scale to estimate an industry funding shortfall or crash date. Full inputs and monthly recalculations are in this update's `calculations.json`.

### 14.7 Contemporaneous Regulation: Reviewing Specific Equipment, Users and End Uses

BIS announced an interim final rule on remote compute on November 16. The Federal Register public inspection page schedules publication for November 19. As of this chapter's research date it has not been formally published; formal publication triggers effectiveness, so it cannot be described as fully implemented on November 16. [BIS announcement](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/bis-issues-rule-addressing-remote-access-american-super-intelligence-technology), [public inspection status](https://www.federalregister.gov/public-inspection/2026-23529/addressing-remote-access-to-advanced-semiconductors)

Two layers of review matter most for operations. One concerns new licensing requirements and applicable exclusions for relevant controlled equipment destined for Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Brazil and other destinations. The other concerns which entities ultimately receive what compute from data centers. A location meeting one exclusion does not exempt the end user or end use from review; insufficient customer due-diligence information may also cause delivery or approval delays first. The text includes thresholds, exceptions for specified uses and transition provisions, all of which still require transaction-by-transaction examination against the final version. [Public inspection rule, §742.6 and §744.24](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-23529.pdf)

The recommendation-system exception does not cover certain generative uses, but **this does not establish that all generative APIs, microdramas or coding applications serving Chinese users are prohibited.** The controlled items, equipment or compute-supply transaction, parties, end uses, scale, knowledge and licensing conditions must first be assessed. Ordinary use of end-user applications cannot be equated with controlled access to data-center compute without analysis.

NVIDIA classifies geographic revenue by the headquarters of direct customers; ultimate customers and delivery locations may differ. Regulation, by contrast, concerns the actual equipment, data centers, end users and parent companies. China's share of direct revenue neither represents all indirect exposure nor means all overseas revenue should be treated as restricted. [Q2 10-Q, geographic and customer classification](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm)

My inference about cash transmission is that delays in customer eligibility or licensing may defer acceptance, lease commencement and loan drawdowns, while equipment, power, lease and debt payments may not move with them. If compliant replacement customers are found, the principal losses may be vacancy periods and migration costs. Only persistent idle capacity would further affect rental prices, collateral valuations and refinancing. Policy should therefore be tracked separately, rather than attributed entirely to Huang's departure or used to apply one uniform revenue reduction across all companies.

### 14.8 Which Links Face Pressure First, and Which Counterevidence Must Be Retained?

| Entity | Changes I would examine first | Conclusions that should not follow automatically |
|---|---|---|
| NVIDIA | Approval of new support, collection of receivables, supply and acceptance, talent and roadmap | High profits do not establish downstream payback; the departure does not mean technology stops functioning |
| Model companies such as OpenAI | Unclosed funding, capacity deployment and guarantee terms, and whether other funders can step in | Tightening one form of support does not mean all funding has broken down |
| Diversified cloud providers | Own cash, long-term commitments, allocation across regions and chip models, and actual delivery of internally developed technology | Legacy-business cash does not establish payback for AI projects; an alternative design is not an achieved share of substitution |
| Compute operators | Major-customer eligibility, lease commencement, loan drawdowns, renewals and fixed payments | Restricted capacity is not permanently idle; prepayments are not necessarily permanently nonrefundable |
| Video, coding and other applications | Costs, service availability, model substitution and user renewals | A supplier's leadership changes do not automatically eliminate real use cases |
| China's substitution ecosystem | Software adaptation, energy use, delivery and customer budgets | A policy opportunity does not directly become an equivalent amount of revenue or profit for a particular company |

I must also take seriously evidence against a financing collapse. Reuters reported on November 16 that partner financial institutions were still discussing projects, with some interviewees saying cooperation continued. The same article also said the platforms had not yet announced transactions. This is sufficient to challenge the claim that "financing has already stopped," but not to establish that USD 500 billion has been deployed. Expectations of greater caution ahead should be separated from actual decisions to suspend funding. [Reuters, November 16](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/Analysis-Nvidias%2BAI%2Bfinancing%2Bmachine%2Bmay%2Bsurvive%2BHuang,%2Bbut%2Bwith%2Bless%2Brisk%2Bappetite/27347181.html)

A stronger countercase is a complete path: succession becomes clear, technology delivery remains stable, multiple sources of capital actually contribute funds, real customer payments continue to grow, and management reduces new low-return commitments. Short-term orders may slow in that case, while future losses decline. Continued financing, halted financing, faster construction and contracting construction cannot all be interpreted as confirming the same prediction.

Competitive substitution also requires testing in both directions. Alternative chips existing does not mean customers can migrate on the same timetable, at the same cost and quality. NVIDIA's installed deployments and engineering ecosystem still have value. If alternative platforms pass acceptance and reduce total costs, diminished competitive advantage on certain tasks should be acknowledged. Technological progress can expand use cases while also shifting profits from the original investors in hardware and models to applications and users. This remains the reason the original report separates technological progress and recovery through customer payments into two axes.

#### 14.8.1 Competition Redistributes Risk; It Does Not Create Payers for the Industry

**I am raising my assessment of the practical substitution capabilities of AMD, TPUs and other alternatives, but do not equate NVIDIA-specific risk with AI industry demand risk, or treat more suppliers as proof that payer credit risk has been diversified.** If the problem is mainly a change in NVIDIA's own delivery or credit support, while customer budgets and funding remain intact, competitors can take on some tasks. If the problem is insufficient collections at the same model companies and cloud customers, changing chips cannot automatically fill the budget gap. The original report already covered TPU system sales, AMD warrants and Broadcom notes. This update examines how they jointly change orders, profit and risk-bearing; it does not claim those facts were previously absent. [AMD customer-support disclosures](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2488/000000248826000191/amd-20260926.htm), [Anthropic's multiple-supplier contracts](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026072484/anthropicpbc-sx1ax2.htm)

First assess the product and distribution status documented as of November 17. Announcements, limited adoption, production use, general rental availability and completed deployment of an entire tranche are different stages. The table does not treat vendor performance claims as measured rankings at equal quality, or interpret general availability as unlimited supply in every region.

#### 14.8.2 Moving from a Running Workload to Retained Profit Still Requires Engineering

The strong case for AMD now has customer-side evidence. On October 15, Meta published research on production training of recommendation models using MI350X. Across ten representative workloads, with GPU counts and batch sizes matched, geometric-mean throughput was approximately 101.2% of B200's. But the MI350X and B200 power limits in Meta's deployment were 1000W and 750W respectively, and the study concerned recommendation models. It cannot establish a universal advantage in all large-model training, at equal power or at equal cost. The paper also describes changes to operators, memory layouts, communications, fault detection and monitoring. The results show that engineering migration is feasible, and that this investment cannot be omitted. [Meta research paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22230v1)

ROCm, PyTorch and vLLM are lowering entry barriers. Google's October release of TorchTPU also brings native PyTorch to TPUs. My inference is that large customers can spread migration investment across subsequent procurement, challenging the pricing protection afforded by a single software ecosystem. But framework support does not mean custom CUDA operators, quantization precision, dynamic shapes and distributed communications all need no rework. vLLM requires compatible software builds, while TorchTPU retains compiler and custom-kernel constraints. More accelerator memory becomes a real economic advantage only when it reduces partitioning, transfers or recomputation. [vLLM installation requirements](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/getting_started/installation/gpu/), [PyTorch's TorchTPU explanation](https://pytorch.org/blog/introducing-torchtpu/)

Comparisons should hold constant the model and quality threshold, input and output lengths, numerical precision, concurrency, latency percentiles, availability SLA and test date. For training, assess effective time to reach the same quality. For inference, assess the full cost of accepted tasks, including equipment or rental, power, host systems and networking, storage, migration staff and failed runs that must be repeated. A low list price accompanied by poor utilization or more failures may not be cheap; a more expensive platform may retain customers by reducing migration work and time to production. These are acceptance criteria based on public production cases. No public, uniform evaluation supports an overall ranking of every platform. [Meta discussion of production and reliability](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22230v1)

**AMD can win orders while facing profit pressure.** Customers may genuinely shift orders, or simply use a second supplier's quote to force NVIDIA to concede on price. Total market procurement, market share, transaction prices and support costs jointly determine AMD's results. Its Q3 Data Center revenue growth also includes EPYC and cannot all be counted as GPU orders won from rivals. If total budgets decline while price cuts and engineering investment increase, rising share can coexist with falling revenue or profit. This is the strong countercase that optimism about competition must confront. [AMD segment scope](https://newsroom.amd.com/news/amd-reports-third-quarter-2026-earnings/)

Value capture from TPUs requires four separate accounts: savings in Google's internal services, Cloud fees, complete-system sales, and model and application revenue. Internal savings are not external chip revenue, and total Cloud profit is not TPU profit; Alphabet has not separately disclosed TPU product financials. Cloud providers may retain the benefit if prices hold; if competition passes savings to model providers or application customers, the benefit moves downstream. System sales may also reduce Google's own construction investment while increasing construction and financing at customers buying those systems. A fall in one company's CapEx alone cannot establish an industry-wide slowdown. [Alphabet Q3 discussion](https://abc.xyz/investor/events/event-details/2026/2026-Q3-Earnings-Call-2026-Ujou16CTw9/), [system-sales accounting](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000084/goog-20260930.htm)

Broadcom has another way to benefit: it supplies custom accelerators and interconnect networking, so winning orders need not depend every time on one GPU brand prevailing. Its recognized AI semiconductor revenue, however, combines these businesses; it cannot all be classified as ASIC revenue, much less used to infer standalone profit for a particular chip. Customization suited to stable tasks at scale may avoid the cost of general-purpose capabilities. If models, scale or deployment requirements change, redesign and software adaptation can also consume those savings. This is a judgment about business mechanics, not an assumption of uniform gross-margin or residual-value advantages for custom chips. [Broadcom Q3 business disclosures](https://investors.broadcom.com/news-releases/news-release-details/broadcom-inc-announces-third-quarter-fiscal-year-2026-financial)

#### 14.8.4 Different Chips Still Face Shared Bottlenecks and Capital-Recovery Tests

Competitors also rely on advanced fabrication, packaging, HBM, data centers and power, while many workloads need common networking and host components. Orders NVIDIA does not win may go to AMD or custom chips without reducing upstream utilization. But if ultimate customers delay together, upstream expansion, inventory and committed procurement also come under pressure. Technological substitutability, securing wafers, completing systems, connection to power and acceptance, and customer payment are five different stages. Without public evidence about capacity allocation and adaptation, one cannot assume that a customer's order cut allows the original capacity to shift immediately and without loss to another model. [AMD supply-chain risks](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2488/000000248826000191/amd-20260926.htm), [TSMC Q3 discussion](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-10/eba4fac72873bf546b669bd2b5d973df5c93215d/TSMC%203Q26%20Transcript.pdf)

Market-access risk likewise does not disappear with a change of brand. US vendors' GPUs and ASICs still require licensing review based on the specific equipment, users and end uses. Opportunities for Chinese systems must also pass through software adaptation, supply, customer budgets and collections. Technological diversity may reduce exposure to a single vendor's failure, but does not necessarily diversify regional policy shocks or shocks to common ultimate customers. This discussion retains the regulatory status as of November 17 set out in section 14.7: public inspection is not effectiveness, and does not establish a ban on all APIs. [Public inspection rule](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-23529.pdf)

Older capital requires a separate test. If new platforms complete tasks of equal quality at lower cost, rental prices and residual values for older GPUs may be revised downward first, while equipment loans and minimum payments do not fall in step. New ASIC investment may continue, potentially expanding the industry's total capital still to be recovered. Conversely, routing, fine-tuning and lighter inference can redeploy older equipment to suitable tasks, and lower prices may expand paid demand. Both paths are consistent with technological progress. Subsequent net cash from the same equipment cohort must be compared; new-platform revenue cannot establish payback for shareholders in older equipment. [AWS on demand for older generations](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-custom-chips-demand-q3-2026-earnings), [equipment leases and financing conditions](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026072484/anthropicpbc-sx1ax2.htm)

#### 14.8.5 When Competition Helps Absorption, and When It Amplifies Risk After a Delay

The table presents the author's conditional judgments, not probability forecasts. Real production and supplier support coexist, leading me to retain three paths. The basis remains the product, customer-contract and credit-support evidence above, rather than competing vendors' stock prices.

| Scenario | Possible outcome | Counterevidence that would weaken the judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Only NVIDIA's delivery or new support comes under pressure; customer funding remains intact | Available alternatives take on some tasks, shifting share and bargaining power; costs may initially rise during migration | Alternative platforms persistently fail acceptance at equal quality, or customer funding deteriorates too |
| Costs at equal quality fall, with sufficient growth in paid tasks and utilization | Savings become customer benefits and net operating cash; older equipment continues paying back and the industry absorbs investment in an orderly way | Fee declines exceed usage growth; vacancy rises, while renewals and gross profit on a cash basis for older assets deteriorate |
| Common customers' recovery is insufficient while support and duplicate construction keep expanding | AMD may gain share while profit falls; GPU and ASIC owners jointly face payment and residual-value pressure, and continued financing may amplify risk after a delay | Customer operating budgets replace subsidies, net firm obligations fall, and cash from older assets and financing terms keep improving |

My observation sequence is to establish that tasks meeting the same quality standard have genuinely entered production and been accepted by customers, then examine transaction prices, renewals and net collections, followed by actual capital contributions or drawdowns, whether obligations can be released and whether guarantees are triggered. Only then do I assess whether multiple entities are contracting together. If only one chip vendor's sales change, I first examine market share, product transitions, inventory and other factors, rather than directly attribute it to industry-wide contraction. I escalate the industry judgment only if customers across multiple platforms delay orders, collections weaken and financing tightens together. This sequence also accommodates the countercase: if capital support bridges construction and operating cash subsequently takes over, I should acknowledge that competition is helping absorb investment. [Customer delivery and financing conditions](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026072484/anthropicpbc-sx1ax2.htm)

**The new research therefore changes the distribution of risk and what must be verified; competing products' progress alone does not reset the date of a bubble burst.** Cheaper compute can benefit applications and users while putting older capital under pressure. If operating collections catch up with investment, the pessimistic assessment should be reduced. Continued financing supports the judgment that "delay amplifies risk" only when net firm obligations grow faster still. The main window and accountability for an unfulfilled forecast on June 30, 2028 remain in place, without new share forecasts, price targets or uncalibrated probabilities.

### 14.9 Evidence for Updating the Judgment, Rather Than Treating an Event as Fulfillment of the Forecast

The first layer is scheduled disclosures. FY27Q3 results were scheduled for approximately 13:20 US Pacific time on November 17, or approximately 05:20 on November 18 Beijing time, with the earnings call at 06:00 Beijing time. This chapter does not cite results that have not yet been released. Next I will examine succession authority, investment and guarantee policies, supply and customer support commitments, product delivery and subsequent collections. [Results timing announcement](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition)

The second layer is implementation of the rule and project-by-project performance: the final Federal Register publication, actual licenses, replacement customers, acceptance and drawdown conditions. If payments can be deferred in step, or compliant customers take over promptly, the assessment of pressure should be reduced. The amplification mechanism is supported only when operating cash is delayed while firm payments continue.

The third layer is successive financial reports and contracts after the event. At least a joint deterioration in collections, renewal prices, credit facilities, actual capital contributions or orders across multiple entities, with traceable transmission, is needed to move closer to an industry adjustment. A DSO increase alone, one stock-price decline, negative cash after construction spending or analysts' speculation is insufficient. Conversely, completed financings, higher net collections from older equipment, and fewer commitments alongside stable delivery are counterevidence that must be acknowledged.

Over a longer horizon, tests follow product and renewal milestones: whether the technology team continues to deliver, whether customers actually migrate and whether older assets pay back as planned. These are levels of review, not a new set of dates on which a burst must occur. My main window and responsibility to record it as unfulfilled on June 30, 2028 if it does not materialize remain unchanged. If future evidence is insufficient, I must acknowledge that the judgment has not been supported rather than automatically roll the window forward.

**This update makes me place greater weight on governance at key financing nodes, but does not overturn the nine core judgments: AI can create real value, application markets can expand, and suppliers can earn profits. Ultimately, customer cash must still be tested against recovery of investment, entity by entity, contract by contract and asset cohort by asset cohort.**
