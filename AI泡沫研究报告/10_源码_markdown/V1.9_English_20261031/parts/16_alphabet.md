## Appendix D: Alphabet's Financing Case—From Planned Capacity to Per-Share Value

Information cutoff: 2026-10-31 (Beijing time). All amounts are in USD billions; share prices and earnings per share are in USD. The Q3 earnings release and September 30 10-Q have been published; the interim financial statements are unaudited. ATM issuance, bond financing and cash balances have been rolled forward from the Q2 baseline to Q3. Announced plans, completed issuance, actual cash and future optional capacity remain separately presented.

### D.1 The USD 80 Billion Plan Exists, but It Is Not USD 80 Billion Received at Once

This round of Alphabet financing has moved from planning into further execution: the initial 6/1 plan had an 80 ceiling; common and preferred stock financing generated net cash of 49.562 in June; Q3 brought a further 12.371 in net cash through the ATM program; and net cash from the two types of equity financing totaled 61.933 for the first nine months. Approximately 27.6 of ATM capacity remained as of 9/30. New shares, preferred-stock rights and debt interest have all entered existing shareholders' cash and per-share models. The current position can no longer be described as "the ATM has not yet been used."

The structure in the formal 6/1 filing comprised an underwritten offering of 30, an ATM program of 40 and a private placement of 10 to Berkshire affiliates; the underwritten portion included common stock and mandatory convertible preferred stock. This document establishes the financing plan at that time, not full execution, and still less that management determined operating cash would be insufficient for the next three years. ### D.2 Ask "What Is the New Money For?" and Check "What Is the Existing Money?"

As of 9/30, Alphabet had cash and cash equivalents of 56.334 and marketable securities of 208.764, totaling 265.098. The securities included equity investments of 90.384, of which 75.2 was SpaceX stock subject to short-term sale restrictions. Cash, government and debt securities, and restricted equity are not the same form of liquidity. The figure 265.098 can describe the reported combined assets, but cannot all be called cash immediately available to pay construction bills. The carrying value of long-term debt of 125.102 at the same date also differs from note principal and includes the effects of discounts, hedges and current-portion classification. [Q3 10-Q, balance sheet, investment and debt notes](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000084/goog-20260930.htm)

The operating cash-timing pressure has continued beyond a single negative quarter: Q3 operating cash of 42.290 and cash capital expenditures of 51.355 produced FCF of −9.065, more negative than Q2's −5.855; trailing-twelve-month FCF remained +19.747. Cloud operating profit rose to 11.121 over the same period, showing that operating demand and group-level asset-payment pressure can rise simultaneously. Cloud profit cannot prove that every new batch of assets has paid back, nor can a negative group quarter be permanently extrapolated into an operating breakdown. [Official Q3 financial results and FCF reconciliation](https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_financials/2026/q3/2026q3-alphabet-earnings-release.pdf)

Uses of proceeds should continue to be recorded separately according to the documents. Completed common- and preferred-stock proceeds are for general corporate purposes, including AI infrastructure; the ATM program is primarily for tax obligations related to employee equity awards. Q3 ATM execution does not change this distinction. Neither the 12.371 received nor the remaining 27.6 of capacity can therefore be allocated entirely to new AI data centers, and the entire group's cash shortfall cannot be attributed to AI.

Financing may simultaneously support construction, cash management, acquisitions and capital-structure choices. Only by aligning these uses with cash balances, debt maturities, operating expenditure and shareholder returns can one judge whether financing proactively adds headroom or fills a gap for payments that cannot be postponed. Issuing shares alone does not yield a unique answer.

### D.3 For Existing Shareholders, the Real Calculation Is Per-Share Value

Financing expands company assets but also adds claims for new shareholders or holders of preferred rights. Looking only at the amount raised or the increase in shares shows just one side. The test is whether the future cash purchased with those funds can exceed the additional claims and financing costs.

#### D.3.1 Common Stock: More Shares Do Not Automatically Mean Value Destruction

In a simplified model excluding preferred stock, let pre-financing common-equity value be V₀, the share count N₀, net issuance cash C, newly issued shares ΔN, and the net present value created by new projects after deducting investment NPV. Then:

> Post-financing value per share =（V₀ + C + NPV）/（N₀ + ΔN）.

If the proceeds remain on the balance sheet, first include them as cash; if they are invested in projects, project NPV must already deduct the investment, so that cash and the same project asset are not counted twice. Issuance expenses, taxes and control-right differences should also be included. Do not simply divide the financing amount by total market capitalization to claim a permanent percentage loss.

A purely illustrative example: initial value is 1000 and the share count 10, giving pre-financing value of 100 per share. Issuing 0.1 new shares at 100 each raises cash of 10. If project NPV is zero, post-financing value of 1010/10.1 remains 100 per share. Although each share owns a smaller proportion of the company, no value is lost under this no-cost assumption. If new-project NPV is −5, value per share is approximately 99.50; if it is +5, approximately 100.50. This is not an Alphabet price target; it only illustrates why financing price and project returns must be calculated together.

The June public common-stock offering and private placement added 86 million shares, and the Q3 ATM issued another 36 million. Their combined 122 million shares can be used as the issuance volume for these specific financing transactions. It cannot be added again to the net increase in common shares for the entire period, which also includes employee shares, repurchases and other items. Period-end common shares at 9/30 were 12.294 billion, while Q3 diluted weighted-average shares were 12.368 billion. They measure different populations and time weights; the quarter's EPS cannot be reverse-engineered directly from the increase in period-end shares. [Q3 10-Q, equity and EPS notes](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000084/goog-20260930.htm)

#### D.3.2 Mandatory Convertible Preferred Stock: Check Dividends and Conversion Terms First

The preferred stock carries a 6.25% dividend, payable in cash, common stock or a combination at the company's option. Mandatory conversion normally occurs in May 2029, with the share count depending on the price range of the relevant share class and anti-dilution provisions. The company also paid approximately 1.0 for a capped call to reduce some conversion dilution. This is not cost-free funding, nor can it be broadly described as "no current dilution and no additional burden ever." [Q2 10-Q, Note 11 and earnings-per-share note](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000071/goog-20260630.htm)

Q3 recognized preferred-stock dividends of 0.298; subtracting these from net income of 26.468 left income attributable to common stock of 26.170. This shows that preferred rights already affect the current common-share earnings measure; one cannot focus only on the share count at future conversion. This is also a separate item from non-operating gains and losses caused by fair-value changes in the company's equity securities, and each should enter the per-share bridge separately. [Q3 10-Q, earnings-per-share note](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000084/goog-20260930.htm)

The research model should separately list current preferred dividends, conversion share counts, the capped call's effective range and cost, and follow the EPS note's anti-dilution treatment. Without a tranche-by-tranche model, no reliable range for permanent dilution or future EPS changes can be provided.

| Financing instrument | Rights and cash to list first | Position in the existing-shareholder model |
|---|---|---|
| Common stock | Net cash proceeds, new shares, issue price and expenses | Cash or project value enters the numerator; additional shares enter the denominator |
| Mandatory convertible preferred stock | Cumulative dividends, conversion date, price range, anti-dilution and capped call | Periodic dividends and future conversion shares, with EPS rules checked separately |
| ATM capacity | Actual issuance dates, shares, net proceeds and remaining capacity | No cash received recorded before execution; no assumption of all dilution at once |
| Fixed-rate bonds | Net proceeds, coupons, maturities and covenants | Interest, debt-repayment cash and financing repricing; no direct addition to common shares |
| Floating-rate loans | Reference rate, spread, repricing date and balance | Rate changes flow through on contractual dates; policy rates are not applied directly |

Bond financing adds interest and principal due at maturity, but does not directly increase common shares. Common stock, preferred stock and bonds cannot be placed in a universally valid ranking "from cheapest to most expensive": financing timing, issue price, tax shields, rating constraints and investment returns all change the actual cost.

### D.4 Evidence Boundaries for External Investor Participation

This review reread Note 11 of the Q2 10-Q and confirmed Berkshire affiliates' participation in the private placement of 10. It is an external funding source. Investing in Alphabet does not turn it into an internal loop between cloud providers and model companies, still less an "endorsement at an above-fair-value price" without a pricing comparison.

This investment establishes only that an institution accepted the relevant price and rights. It does not prove optimism about all AI assets, nor replace a calculation of Alphabet's project returns. Who participated in the decision, why this company was selected, and whether other companies were reduced at the same time each require their own public statements or holdings evidence. Inferring from a single private placement that a particular individual has concluded other giants will collapse is not a verifiable conclusion.

Further research on Berkshire should obtain quarterly 13Fs, check share classes, share counts and reporting dates, and separate purchases from changes in the market value of holdings. Quarter-end disclosures still do not equal real-time positions on October 31, and actual investment cannot be inferred from differences in market value. This appendix does not incorporate a holdings trajectory whose verification remains incomplete.

### D.5 Historical Comparisons Should Compare Rights, Not Guess the Top

Historical financing comparisons must first establish whether the instruments are alike: common stock's residual claim, preferred dividends and conversion terms, upside participation through attached warrants, and debt's repayment at maturity carry different risks. Valuations at financing, counterparty liquidity and investors' bargaining positions also need to be compared together. Preferred rights do not equal risk-free protection, and participation through common stock does not mean an absence of investment value.

Studying the timing ability of financing decisions requires checking original issuance documents transaction by transaction, including issuance cases that were not followed by a crash, and harmonizing event windows, prices or total returns, market benchmarks and sample selection. Searching backward for financing only among companies that subsequently collapsed creates selection bias; it does not establish that Alphabet will follow the same path after financing.

The lessons worth retaining are these: financing addresses the ability to pay but does not automatically solve investment returns; participation by a powerful investor does not eliminate subsequent capital losses; and equity financing may improve debt-paying capacity, making construction more resilient to volatility. Whether it benefits or dilutes existing shareholders must be decided jointly by the issue price, use of proceeds and cash recovery.

The October 28 FOMC decision held the range at 3.75%–4.00%, with two dissenters favoring a 25-basis-point increase; this supersedes the previous edition's "next decision pending" status. Outstanding fixed coupons, floating-rate repricing, future financing and the equity discount rate work through different mechanisms. The policy range cannot be added directly to every Alphabet debt instrument, nor does an unchanged rate mean financing costs have stopped changing. [Official FOMC October 28 decision](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm)

### D.6 How the Next Financial Report Would Change This Appendix's Conclusion

| Subsequent evidence | Where to update | Inference to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Subsequent issuance, shares and expenses against the remaining 27.6 of ATM capacity | Roll forward from the 12.371 already executed in Q3 | Do not treat additional sales agents as additional funds |
| New debt, repayments, floating-rate repricing and debt balances | Continue rolling borrowing cash and maturity schedules from Q3 disclosed receipts | Do not treat a final term sheet as a cash receipt |
| AI assets entering service, Cloud profits and working capital | New-project cash-recovery model | Do not attribute all Cloud profit to assets newly built in that quarter |
| Preferred dividends, conversion and capped-call changes | Share-count and earnings-per-share bridge | Do not book all maximum dilution into the current period at once |
| Cash from commissioned assets consistently exceeding budget | Raise project net present value or shorten payback | Do not maintain a permanently bearish conclusion on the basis of a single financing transaction |
| Revenue delays while mandatory payments and financing costs rise | Lower project net present value and reassess liquidity | Do not directly declare that the company has already defaulted |

This appendix concludes that financing has progressed further into execution: ATM issuance increases common shares, preferred stock generates current dividends, and multicurrency bonds broaden debt funding sources. At the same time, Cloud profit is growing while the group's construction cash shortfall is widening. The initial 80 cannot all be treated as received, but financing can no longer be described merely as a plan. Whether existing shareholders benefit must be decided jointly by issue price, use of proceeds, project net cash and per-share rights. This appendix does not infer a crash date or individual-stock drawdown range from financing size.
