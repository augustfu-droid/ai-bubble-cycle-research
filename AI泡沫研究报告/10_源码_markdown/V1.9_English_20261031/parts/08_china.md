## Chapter 9: Core Implications for Chinese Investors

At least four hurdles separate overseas capital expenditure from Chinese-company profit: product mix, supplier share, delivery timing and cash collection. As of October 31, 2026, Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon continued to report cloud-revenue growth in their new quarters, while formal domestic third-quarter reports showed different cash paths: Zhongji Innolight's Q3 cash rebounded, Eoptolink's Q3 operating cash turned negative, TFC Communication retained a surplus after cash asset purchases over the first three quarters, and Longsys turned positive for the quarter but remained negative cumulatively. Growth has materialized, but cash recovery still requires company-by-company calculation. Total overseas CapEx cannot replace Chinese companies' earnings models. [Zhongji Innolight Q3](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-10-30/1225643953.PDF), [Eoptolink Q3](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-10-30/1225642947.PDF), [TFC Communication Q3](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-10-29/1225634414.PDF)

### 9.1 Identify the Cycle Through Operating Conditions

When projects generate revenue, whether financing can be renewed, and whether investment expenditure can be adjusted proactively explain more than the year itself. A quarter's entry into the research calendar only means new evidence needs to be obtained; if the conditions are unmet, the arrival of a date cannot establish a cyclical turning point.

Cycle assessment separately tracks whether supplier orders and revenue are slowing, whether customer capital expenditure exceeds sustainable cash generation, and whether financing pressure is spreading from individual companies to more credit entities. Strong demand can refute an immediate collapse in the first condition without automatically eliminating the second; a single-name downgrade cannot automatically establish the third.

Model companies' financing windows must also be judged by their actual stage. Media-reported plans, confidential submission, public filing, registration effectiveness and actual issuance are different stages; lockups and share-sale arrangements also require reading formal terms. Planned IPO dates cannot be strung into an inevitable path of capital outflows and market declines.

#### 9.1.1 If the Investment Cycle Lasts Longer Than Expected

Physical constraints can delay delivery, potentially slowing supply expansion and improving the eventual supply-demand balance, but also allowing construction in progress and interest to keep tying up capital. These directions cannot be combined at the outset as "delay inevitably amplifies risk." Each project must be checked: whether customer payment follows delivery, whether equipment can be resold, whether contracts can be canceled, and who ultimately bears construction-period interest.

The opposing-case stress test uses conditions without preset shifts in probabilities. If falling task costs expand demand faster than prices decline, mature businesses keep funding construction, and collections do not deteriorate, the initially cautious cash-recovery judgment should weaken. Conversely, if revenue has not scaled, maintenance capital expenditure has already arrived, and customers depend on new financing to pay, more time increases the capital gap.

Two paths must be distinguished here: gradual disillusionment means returns fall below optimistic pricing and investment and valuations adjust in stages; a soft landing means revenue and efficiency progressively absorb earlier investment as construction slows in an orderly way. Delivery delays may lead to either. This section assigns neither preset probabilities nor position-increase or reduction percentages.

### 9.3 Transmission Through Coding Tools and Software Companies

Per-seat coding-tool revenue covers only part of the software-production tools market. The market boundary changes if companies use saved hours for more projects, adopt usage-priced agents or buy delivery services. Conversely, low individual usage, losses of paid seats or excessive inference costs for frequent users can weaken profits.

"Cheaper tools" therefore proves neither inevitable harm to outsourcing companies nor an unlimited market for tool vendors. A software-company model should at least separate contract value, delivery person-hours, labor costs, tool and compute costs, acceptance cycles, and whether customers demand a share of efficiency gains. Project gross profit can first be built as "contract revenue−delivery labor−tools and compute−other delivery costs," with sales, R&D and administrative expenses deducted separately. Saved hours enter earnings only when they become additional delivery or actual cost reductions; company revenue cannot be inferred directly from the total developer population.

### 9.4 Customer Concentration and Financing Exposure

Customer concentration, affiliated investments and revenue quality should be recorded in separate columns. A supplier's investment in a customer does not prove all subsequent revenue is fictitious; equally, a signed purchase contract does not mean the customer has unconditional cash capacity to perform.

Research working papers should list named customers, recognized revenue, unfulfilled contracts, payment terms, guarantees or credit enhancement, investment balances and cancellation conditions. Researchers must not assign revenue from undisclosed customers to a particular cloud provider, and undisclosed circular-revenue shares should remain missing. Customer concentration and financing links can guide verification, but cannot directly generate a uniform industry risk ranking.

### 9.5 How Company Valuation Carries Through the Industry Judgment

First forecast product revenue, then build profit and cash flow from gross profit, expenses, depreciation, taxes and working capital, and only then discuss valuation multiples. An industry judgment can change these inputs, but cannot bypass the earnings model to produce a precise decline percentage.

For a hypothetical company used solely to illustrate sensitivity: baseline EBITDA is 100, the enterprise-value multiple 20x, net debt 200 and diluted shares 100, giving per-share value of 18. If EBITDA grows 20% to 120 while the multiple falls 25% to 15x, enterprise value becomes 1800 and per-share value 16, down 11.1% from 18. Rising earnings and a declining equity valuation can occur together. These are teaching assumptions, not a real company's price target.

| EBITDA (baseline=100) | 15x | 20x | 25x |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 80 | 10 | 14 | 18 |
| 100 | 13 | 18 | 23 |
| 120 | 16 | 22 | 28 |

All per-share values in the table use "EBITDA×multiple−200, then divide by 100." Net debt and shares are fixed only to isolate two variables. A real model must also handle cash generation, financing and dilution, and use discounted cash flow to test whether terminal value carries too much of the valuation.

### 9.6 Cross-Border Instruments: Channel Rules Cannot Replace Research

Southbound Stock Connect and Greater Bay Area Cross-boundary Wealth Management Connect are different systems. A market daily quota cannot be treated as an individual allocation that can be held or accumulated. HKEX's July 6, 2026 FAQ explains that the daily quota is managed on a net-buy basis and unused portions do not carry forward; holding shares therefore does not generate a personal historical quota that can be saved. [HKEX Stock Connect FAQ, 2026-07-06, pages 15–16](https://www.hkex.com.hk/-/media/HKEX-Market/Mutual-Market/Stock-Connect/Getting-Started/Information-Booklet-and-FAQ/FAQ/FAQ_Cn.pdf)

Exchange-traded QDII premiums must be calculated using comparable NAV, exchange rates and trading prices at the same time. NAV lags across time zones, subscription suspensions and secondary-market liquidity affect the result. Without a comparable same-day sample, no "currently prevailing premium" range can be supplied for the entire market. Fund-size growth or market drawdowns also do not guarantee that premiums reach zero on a particular day.

This report makes no individual determination of readers' cross-border investment eligibility, remittance purposes or applicable products. If trading is needed, those must be checked separately against the latest official rules and product documents, rather than arranging channels or positions in advance based on a research view.

### 9.8 Attributing Policy Shocks

Export controls affect procurable products, deployment locations and technology-migration costs; capital-market regulation affects trading channels. They cannot be combined into one "AI risk event." For a specific company, check in sequence whether the product falls within the rule, licensing conditions, actual deliverable timing and alternatives, then adjust volume, cost and lead times. A policy discussion draft is not an effective rule, and authorization to procure is not delivery. Regulatory documents must be retained for penalty amounts, product bans and effective dates; unverified amounts are not entered as risk losses.

### 9.9 Where This Chapter May Be Wrong

Supplier-share gains and product upgrades may offset slower industry investment, the opposing case most easily missed by macro extrapolation. Working capital is also seasonal; one quarter or cumulative first-three-quarter cash cannot establish a full-year conclusion. At the same time, genuine growth does not protect an excessive entry valuation, and policy support does not guarantee adoption of an individual company's products. The next verification should allow all these directions into the model, rather than seek only numbers supporting the original view.
