## Appendix E: Fact Checks and Evidence Boundaries

This table supports the current V1.9 judgments, with information through 31 October 2026, Beijing time; interest rates use US observations for 30 October. F01–F16 carry forward the verified V1.8 baseline; this review deepens video-model, microdrama-duration, and coding-labor-cost research. A higher version number does not mean every historical source was reacquired or quarterly financial statements externally audited. Unless otherwise stated, B means billion and M means million.

The body distinguishes disclosed facts, company forward-looking statements, third-party estimates, the author's conditional illustrations, and unverified matters. Company IR materials, SEC statements, and republications can cross-check excerpts, but one disclosing entity does not create several independent sets of operating evidence. Cross-review tests reasoning; it does not replace auditors or evidence of end-customer cash.

### E.3 Claims Excluded from Current Conclusions

- Industry-wide AI returns without independent evidence of end-customer payments, or undeduplicated totals of circular financing, procurement, and guarantees.
- Inferring realized revenue from downloads, plays, popularity, or surveyed willingness to pay; inferring an AI-generation market from all microdramas; calling global or ex-China data US data.
- Directly translating lower production cost into higher margins while omitting acquisition, refunds, failed productions, labor, and rights costs; replacing industry averages with platform-reported hits.
- Presenting company guidance, third-party forecasts, annualized run rates, or scenario illustrations as realized financial results; concealing single-source access restrictions as “multiple-party verification.”
- Declaring insolvency or collapsing demand directly from negative FCF; proving capital returns from completed financing, an IPO filing, or a large contract.
- Generating new target prices, default probabilities, and crash dates from old prices, currently unverified CDS, or arbitrary scenario weights.
- Retrofitting old conditional seat illustrations into forecasts with specific target years, or rewriting the original draft's longstanding acknowledgment of charges across fields as “we once forecast that only coding could earn money.”
- Generalizing subproduct ARR, group revenue, user activity, or court-restructuring status to an entire industry; conflating API promotions, restricted previews, open weights, and unconditional commercial licenses.
- Renaming mobile-app usage per user as total film viewing across all devices; calculating video-model generation demand from repeat viewing.
- Treating work output, jobs, wages, resource value, and avoidable cash as one metric; upgrading observational employment gaps into AI-caused layoff proportions.
