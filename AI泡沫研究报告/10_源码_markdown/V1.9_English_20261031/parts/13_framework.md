## Appendix A: Research Framework and Methodology

### A Framework for Studying the AI Industry Cycle: Financing Links, Physical Delivery and Capital Recovery

This framework tracks a sequence of conversions that must happen on time, rather than an ever-larger CapEx check: can financing buy equipment, can equipment receive power and be delivered, can delivery generate paid use, and can paid use leave sufficient cash? A slowdown anywhere can disconnect earlier orders/revenue from later capital returns. Conversely, pressure at one link does not directly prove that end demand across the industry has disappeared.

This appendix retains the analytical framework and dated examples from the 27 October 2026 edition. New company earnings, financing and macro inputs on 28—30 October appear in this edition's earnings, financing, valuation and overall-conclusions chapters; old examples do not automatically become 31 October current values. Historical cases test mechanisms, retain original years and do not serve as current industry statistics. This is a research tool, not a frequency-calibrated “AI crash probability model.”

### I. Research Methodology

#### Label Evidence Sources Separately from the Nature of Claims

One company document may contain recognized revenue, unpaid contracts, future guidance and risk warnings. Coming from a securities-regulatory system does not turn all four into realized facts. Each core figure needs two labels: source traceability, and whether the claim is fact, forecast, assumption or judgment.

Annualized revenue per megawatt for selected short contracts cannot be multiplied by all committed power: contract duration, commissioning rates and customer prices differ. Convertible principal, proceeds after discounts and available cash after hedging costs are likewise not interchangeable. Establish numerators, denominators and cash classifications before arithmetic, avoiding precise but meaningless totals.

| Nature of claim | Original information to preserve | What it cannot replace |
|---|---|---|
| Realized fact | Reporting period, accounting basis, currency, notes or announcement terms | One quarter cannot directly become a future full year |
| Company plan/guidance | Release date, applicable period, scope, conditions and later revisions | Not payments or deliveries already made |
| External forecast | Institution, model sample, period, scenario and key assumptions | Not company commitments or market facts |
| Author judgment | Supporting facts, derivation, counterevidence and missing variables | Source count cannot substitute for valid reasoning |

Original contracts, regulatory filings, official earnings and statistical-agency materials take priority for terms and realized data; media and research institutions provide event leads, expert interpretation and forecast references. Traceable primary sources are preferred, but disclosure may still not reveal ultimate payers, customer gross profit or project payback; these gaps must remain explicit.

Cross-institution reconciliation requires more than “all being three-star evidence”: entity, period, currency, cash/noncash classification, gross/net basis and transaction boundaries must align. Three institutions may use the same company guidance; three forecasts citing shared information are not three independent pieces of evidence.

#### Separate Financial Substance before Aggregating Exposure

| Type | Terms to verify | Ledger treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Equity and loans | Actual payment, staged closing, undrawn balances, maturity and recovery conditions | Separate paid assets from unpaid commitments |
| Equipment/cloud purchases | Minimum purchases, cancellation rights, acceptance and settlement progress | Separate contractual obligations from actual procurement/payments |
| Prepayments | Refundability, credit against future bills and recipient-use restrictions | Cash has arrived, but revenue is not necessarily recognized |
| Residual guarantees/credit support | Triggers, maximum liability, duration, indemnification rights and collateral | Contingent exposure, not current cash spending |
| Warrants | Vesting/exercise, fair value, accounting classification and potential dilution | Review with procurement incentives and statement effects |

Equity may arrive in installments over years, while cloud contracts may span hardware replacement cycles. Buyer CapEx and seller equipment revenue are two sides of one transaction. Adding ultimate customers' application payments requires first drawing industry boundaries; otherwise money passing through three links becomes three falsely independent demands.

### II. Financing Networks: From “Large Numbers” to Cash Dependence and Contingent Obligations

#### Linked Structures Do Not Mean False Transactions

A supplier investing in a customer that buys its equipment first establishes intertwined financing/business relationships. This may reduce procurement uncertainty and accelerate real capacity, or make sales growth dependent on continuing finance. Reciprocal contracts, similar amounts and overlapping identities justify examination, but do not prove investment fully recirculated, much less fraud.

Distinguish three layers: disclosed relationships, individually reconcilable funds flows, and who bears loss after customer default. Only linking payments, collections, contract performance and ultimate external-customer revenue establishes whether end cash can sustain the network.

#### Two Verified Cases: Guarantees and Warrants Are Not Cash Investment

NVIDIA's 17 August 2026 8-K discloses that OpenAI is the tenant at SB Energy's Portsmouth project. The initial residual-value guarantee concerns approximately 4.25GW of IT load, with cumulative payment liability capped at USD 105 billion, conditional on service readiness and other requirements expected to be met from 2028. It covers the difference between an agreed minimum value after default and recoveries through replacement leasing or sale, with indemnification and termination terms. It is therefore a long-term contingent obligation to monitor, not USD 105 billion NVIDIA has already invested or an equal expected loss. [NVIDIA 2026-08-17 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000069/nvda-20260817.htm)

AMD's OpenAI arrangement combines procurement with equity incentives. The October 2025 disclosure of warrants for up to 160 million shares at USD 0.01 each includes staged vesting tied to procurement milestones, share prices and technical/commercial conditions. The explicit initial binding purchase commitment is 1GW; full vesting requires corresponding purchases up to 6GW and other conditions. The full ultimate target cannot be treated as orders already delivered. [AMD 2025-10-06 8-K](https://ir.amd.com/financial-information/sec-filings/content/0001193125-25-230895/d28189d8k.htm)

Accounting also goes beyond “no cash, only future dilution.” AMD's 2025 third-quarter filing says grant-date warrant fair value reduces related revenue as recognized; fair-value changes while liability-classified enter other income/expense. Its 2026 second-quarter filing says the OpenAI and Meta warrants, each up to 160 million shares, were unvested/unexercisable at 27 June, with no quarterly/half-year statement effect, and were classified as liabilities pending satisfaction of the equity-classification conditions. The first describes treatment, the second period-end status; neither means “no revenue effect forever.” [AMD 2025Q3 10-Q, note 15](https://ir.amd.com/financial-information/sec-filings/content/0000002488-25-000166/amd-20250927.htm); [AMD 2026Q2 10-Q, Warrants](https://ir.amd.com/financial-information/sec-filings/content/0000002488-26-000123/amd-20260627.htm)

These cases require two additional model schedules: quarterly contractual cash flows, and payments/equity changes contingent on specified conditions. “No current effect” cannot exclude future risk, while “large future ceilings” cannot prove present losses.

#### Three Risk Concentrations

**First, does customer purchasing power depend on the next funding round?** Track nonfinancing customer collections, operating consumption, available cash, committed facilities and procurement/debt payments due over the next twelve months. New funding eases liquidity but cannot replace better customer unit economics. If payments depend on continued shareholder contributions, record that dependence rather than treat all new orders as mature end demand.

**Second, can equipment/projects become billable capacity within financing maturities?** Equipment payments, construction, grid connection, acceptance and lease commencement may fall to different companies. Delays need not cancel final demand but prolong cash-flow gaps. Recoverable values differ across GPUs, cooling, land, power-connection rights and leases; one depreciation life cannot represent them all.

**Third, does default return risk upstream?** Offtake, guarantees, prepayments, repurchase or indemnification clauses may transmit customer credit risk to equipment suppliers. Stress analysis needs default triggers, replacement leasing, collateral recovery and realizability of indemnification, distinguishing nominal contractual ceilings, recognized accounting amounts and scenario losses.

For a purely illustrative shortfall guarantee, let guaranteed value be G, realizable net post-default recovery R and liability ceiling L. Conditional payment can initially be estimated as `min(L, max(0, G − R))`; real contracts also require amounts already paid, performance conditions and termination terms. Multiplying the ceiling by an uncalibrated probability cannot produce a credible precise industry-loss forecast.

#### What to Retain from Telecom-Equipment Financing Comparisons

The comparison should concern supplier financing, customer credit and equipment residual value. Financing numerators must distinguish loans, equity, offtake and guarantees; revenue denominators must match entities/periods. Without a consistent sample, scale multiples do not measure crisis severity.

Compare who pays customers, whether revenue requires delivery acceptance, whether upstream parties backstop losses, debt maturities, equipment resale and how many new projects core cash can support. Mature cloud-business cash and emerging compute operators' financing conditions differ substantially and require separate layers of analysis. The weakest participant's financing pressure does not establish simultaneous industry-wide default.

### III. The Physical Supply Chain: Deliverable Capacity Explains Revenue Timing

#### HBM and Packaging: Different Denominators Are Not Interchangeable

High-bandwidth memory (HBM) capacity per unit, bandwidth, yield and supply terms affect accelerator costs/delivery. But HBM's share of a GPU bill of materials, server purchase-price composition and cloud providers' total CapEx are three different questions. Public data cannot reconstruct uniform industry-wide bills of materials and pure-AI CapEx denominators, so this framework supplies no deceptively precise industry-wide HBM percentage.

Retain the following cost-transmission model without predetermining who earns the profits:

`Server procurement cost = accelerators and associated boards + CPU/memory + networking + racks and other components`.

`Project capital expenditure = server/network procurement + facility, electrical and cooling construction + other project costs requiring capitalization`.

If accelerator prices already include HBM/advanced packaging, memory and packaging vendors' sales cannot be added again to project investment. Assessing HBM providers' profit expansion requires product mix, bargaining mechanisms, unit costs and new capacity, not only revenue or company-wide gross margin. Physical bottlenecks can redistribute supply-chain profits but do not themselves prove circular financing.

#### Power Constraints: Units and Geographic Boundaries

Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory's June 2026 United States Data Center Energy Usage Report: 2025 Update gives a 2030 reference scenario of 649TWh annual consumption, approximately 11.8% of US electricity, and a combined uncertainty range of 521–843TWh, or 9.5%–15.3%. This forecasts multiple data-center types, not actual pure-AI consumption or contracted power demand. The reference case depends on equipment shipments, operations, cooling and other inputs; “2025” in the title does not mean the projected endpoint has already occurred. [LBNL official report page, June 2026](https://datacenters.lbl.gov/publications/united-states-data-center-energy-2025)

TWh is energy over a period; GW is power. Dividing 649TWh by 8760 annual hours gives average load of approximately 74.1GW, still not facility nameplate power, IT capacity or grid-connectable capacity. Peaks/troughs, utilization, cooling and local reliability affect capacity needs; differently defined power figures cannot simply be subtracted into a national shortage.

The US forecast and IEA's global 2030 central projection of 950TWh differ in institution, release date and assumptions and lack reconciled regional components. Dividing 649 by 950 does not directly establish US share or combine them into a regional table. The former informs US sensitivities, the latter global direction; differences remain model-reconciliation items. [IEA April 2026 forecast](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary)

National totals indicate potentially large demand; timely project delivery depends on the local grid. Five project dates are more explanatory: secured power agreement, key-equipment delivery, construction completion, grid-connection acceptance and customer billing commencement. Behind-the-meter (BTM) generation, storage or cross-region dispatch may help, but involve fuel, emissions permits, backup and reliability. All standby gas-turbine capacity is not year-round supply capacity.

Higher power prices need not pass one-for-one into API prices. Operators may absorb them through efficiency, routing and product mix, or lower margins. Durable pass-through requires demand and bargaining power. Examine equal-quality task charges, energy per task and delivery costs together.

#### Advanced Packaging: From “Industry-Wide Queues” to Technology-Specific Delivery

CoWoS and other advanced packaging remain important, but technology type, capacity definition, actual output, yields, customer qualification and product size need separate records. Wafer counts, package counts and deliverable accelerators are different units. Larger products may consume more process resources, and monthly nominal capacity growth need not produce proportionate deliveries.

Capacity reservations, delivery waits and order-taking suspension are different statuses. Without original order rules, applicable products and lines, one customer's schedule cannot establish an industry-wide stop in orders; third-party capacity forecasts are not TSMC realized output.

For each affected company, ask four questions: which packaging is secured, where the delivery bottleneck lies, which quarter's revenue delay affects, and whether alternative processes or acceptance conditions exist. Pre-delivery inventory building differs from unsold finished goods; customer/supplier prepayments and equipment arrivals help establish actual execution.

### IV. Three-Layer CapEx Analysis and Cash-Recovery Models

#### Three Layers and Deduplication

| Layer | Assets studied | Duplication to remove | Cash-recovery clues |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compute/networking | Accelerators, servers, networking and supporting storage | HBM/packaging already included in chip prices are not added again | Billable utilization, effective prices, unit costs and replacement frequency |
| Physical data-center facilities | Buildings, electrical systems, cooling, racks and others | Leased assets and the lessor's same construction expenditure are not counted twice | Powered capacity, lease commencement, occupancy and maintenance CapEx |
| External energy/interconnection | Generation, transmission/distribution, connection and related assets | Separate cloud CapEx from utility investment by entity; PPAs are not entirely CapEx | Commissioning, power contracts, capital-return arrangements and cost bearers |

The global investment total remains unverified. Even sourced institutional forecasts require aligned windows, countries, power/construction inclusion, cash purchases and leased-asset recognition before comparison. Without original reports and breakdowns, retain “unverified”; three institutions agreeing directionally do not supply amount evidence.

Bottom-up calculations build bridges from each company's cash-flow statement, lease notes, guidance and project announcements. Cash asset purchases inform cash financing needs, while new finance-lease assets inform economic investment. If timing differs, avoid adding initial recognition again to future principal payments. Where company “CapEx” definitions differ, display differences before offering comparable subsets.

#### TAM Is Demand Potential, Not Cash Belonging to Investors

Total addressable market (TAM) is potential annual demand under price, user and penetration assumptions, neither actual recognized revenue nor FCF obtainable by equipment purchasers. Application payments may fund cloud, model APIs and equipment; cross-chain revenue cannot be repeatedly summed to cover one layer's capital investment.

Different ramps toward the same terminal annual-revenue target produce different cumulative revenue. Below, terminal annual revenue is 100 currency units, illustrating timing only, not AI market size.

| Assumed revenue path | Five-year cumulative revenue | Example with terminal annual revenue 100 |
|---|---|---|
| Terminal annual revenue R_T achieved from the outset in every year | `5 × R_T` | 500 |
| Continuous linear rise from zero to R_T over five years | `2.5 × R_T` | 250 |
| Five annual values at 20%, 40%, 60%, 80%, 100% of terminal value | `3 × R_T` | 300 |

If initial annual revenue is nonzero, include it explicitly. The continuous-linear general formula is `Cumulative revenue = duration × (initial annualized revenue + terminal annualized revenue) / 2`; discrete annual records must be summed year by year, without mixing continuous areas and year-end values.

#### Returns Must Be Calculated from Periodic Cash Flows

Revenue divided by cumulative CapEx is at most a rough ratio of two same-currency amounts. It deducts no operating costs, taxes or reinvestment and contains no time value. It cannot be named a capital-recovery multiple or establish a negative internal rate of return (IRR). A full model uses one company/project boundary and records genuinely attributable operating earnings/investment each period.

For enterprise free cash flow, one can use:

`FCFF_t = EBIT_t × (1 − tax_rate_t) + depreciation_and_amortization_t − capital_expenditure_t − increase_in_working_capital_t`.

Tax requires separate modeling with losses, deferred tax or tax shields, not mechanically applying a positive-profit rate. Depreciation is deducted in operating income then added back as noncash; after deducting CapEx, it cannot become a second cash investment deduction. FCFF does not additionally deduct financing interest; financing costs enter the matching discount rate. Switching to equity cash flow requires a separate debt/interest schedule.

Net present value (NPV) is `Σ CF_t / (1+k)^t`, including initial investment and later cash flows; IRR is the rate making NPV zero. With multiple cash-flow sign changes, IRR may be nonunique or nonexistent; prioritize NPV at a specified cost of capital. Remaining usable assets/operations can have terminal value, but do not count going-concern terminal value and the same assets' disposal proceeds twice.

Pure illustration: initial investment 100, followed by five year-end net project inflows of 25, with no residual value. Undiscounted inflows total 125, or 1.25 times initial investment; at 10% capital cost, NPV is approximately −5.23 and IRR approximately 7.93%. Even “cumulative cash exceeds investment” does not establish earning the cost of capital, much less returns inferred only from cumulative revenue.

If the initial investment is still paid today but all five payments of 25 are delayed one year, assuming no changes in revenue, costs, residual value or default, NPV falls further to approximately −13.85. Undiscounted cumulative revenue/cash is unchanged, yet value falls approximately 8.62; additional holding costs from delays must be deducted separately. The difference arises from collection timing, requires no assumption of zero end demand, and is not a return estimate for real AI projects.

#### Assess Cash Gaps, RPO and Depreciation Separately

CapEx/operating cash flow (CapEx/OCF), for the same period and positive OCF, measures pressure on operating-cash investment coverage, not default probability. If OCF is negative or near zero, show the cash gap directly rather than let a huge ratio obscure scale. Comparisons should also list cash, upcoming debt maturities, CapEx, dividends/buybacks and secured financing.

`Simplified ending available cash = beginning available cash + OCF − cash capital expenditure − maturing debt principal − dividends/buybacks + secured new financing + other net cash flows`.

“Secured” requires distinguishing signed facilities from actual drawdown conditions. Interest already included in OCF is not deducted again; finance-lease principal outside cash CapEx belongs in separate financing outflows. Unrestricted and restricted cash cannot offset near-term payments equally.

Remaining performance obligations (RPO) mainly represent contracts awaiting revenue recognition, not immediately usable cash. RPO divided by annual OCF has a years dimension; using quarterly OCF requires stating annualization. This appendix has no verified universal automatic-downgrade threshold. Ratings require company-specific duration, cash coverage and methodology; a ratio's threshold cannot be transferred to another metric.

More useful RPO analysis examines recognition timing, performance costs, prepayments, cancellation/renewal and counterparty credit. Depreciation analysis examines lives, replacement speed, impairment and maintenance. CapEx near depreciation proves neither peak construction nor compulsory external financing; it is one observation requiring asset/cash-flow context.

### V. Using the Framework and Recognizing Its Limits

#### Preserve Three Possibilities in One Monitoring Table

| Path to test | Evidence needed together | Signal insufficient alone |
|---|---|---|
| Investment gradually absorbed | Better billable utilization, retention and cash gross profit; falling financing dependence | CapEx continuing to increase alone |
| Demand exists but returns are pressured | Rising use, lower unit charges/effective margins, continuing replacement investment | API cuts or token growth alone |
| Credit and supply contract | Payment/lease delays, deteriorating funding, cancellations or firm-spending cuts corroborate one another | One trading day's share-price decline |

Each update should answer whether new facts alter revenue, costs, timing or financing structure; which parameter should change; and which counterevidence condition was triggered. This avoids selecting only the side consistent with prior beliefs when strong earnings coexist with financing risk.

Evidence tables should also separate “reporting period” and “last verification date.” Rereading a June report in September improves traceability, not turns it into September actuals. Without a financing MOU status update, retain the last verified stage rather than assume closing. Record factual status, model-parameter changes and scenario-weight changes separately, preventing an old announcement newly obtained from becoming another fresh forecast success.

#### Scope and Missing Evidence

The framework suits comparisons of project-recovery paths, earnings sensitivity to demand/prices and participants' cash/maturity mismatches. Public information alone cannot fully identify private-company unit costs, ultimate customer budgets, cancellation rights or bank-credit constraints.

US/Chinese ecosystems differ in financing, product restrictions, energy allocation and customers; one set of ratios cannot transfer directly. Government support, sovereign capital and strategic long-term procurement may change financing duration, but each must be identified as fiscal expenditure, equity, loan, guarantee or policy intention. “National projects” do not automatically receive unlimited backstops.

Still needed are deduplicated transaction funding ledgers, comparable customer cash gross margins, quarterly project lease-commencement/equipment-replacement data, and same-time/same-maturity credit spreads. Without them, offer conditional risk judgments, not one industry recovery rate, crisis date or trading instruction.
